What This Means

This assessment suggests how commercial liability and property lines separate operational risks:

  • BOP (Business Owner's Policy) Bundling: Small businesses can bundle General Liability and Commercial Property into a BOP, often saving 15% to 25% compared to purchasing standalone policies.
  • Personal Auto Exclusions: If an employee drives their personal vehicle to deposit checks, pick up supplies, or visit a client, personal auto policies exclude accidents that occur during business use. Hired & Non-Owned Auto (HNOA) insurance bridges this gap.
  • Statutory vs. Contractual Requirements: While Workers' Comp is legally mandated by state statute when hiring employees, General Liability and Cyber Liability are frequently mandated by commercial leases and client vendor contracts.

Actual insurance requirements vary by state law, municipal regulations, and commercial contracts. Always consult an independent commercial broker or legal counsel.

Important Assumptions

Statutory Grounding

We never declare a policy legally mandated unless supported by explicit state statutes (e.g. Workers' Comp for W-2 staff).

Claims-Made vs Occurrence

Assumes professional liability is written on a claims-made basis requiring continuous retro dates.

Corporate Veil Limits

Recognizes that operating as an LLC or Corp protects against corporate debts, but not individual personal tort liability.

Subcontractor Certificates

Assumes business owners verify Certificates of Insurance (COIs) from all hired 1099 subcontractors.

Things You May Want to Review

1
Client Contract Indemnification Clauses:

Review customer agreements to see if they contractually mandate minimum $1M or $2M E&O and CGL limits.

2
Commercial Lease Insurance Requirements:

Commercial landlords almost universally require tenants to carry $1M General Liability naming the landlord as an Additional Insured.

3
Hired & Non-Owned Auto (HNOA):

If you or employees run business errands in personal cars, ensure HNOA coverage is endorsed onto your general liability policy.

4
Annual Payroll Audit Preparation:

Workers' Comp premiums are adjusted after year-end based on actual audited payroll versus initial estimates.

Frequently Asked Questions: Business Insurance Needs Checklist

Expert mathematical answers, formula explanations, and related comparison guides.

When is Workers' Compensation insurance legally mandatory for a business?
In nearly all US states, Workers' Compensation is legally mandated by statute the moment you hire your first non-owner W-2 employee. Requirements for 1099 independent contractors vary by state labor laws. Audit your commercial risks with our Business Insurance Needs Checklist.
What is the difference between General Liability (CGL) and Professional Liability (E&O)?
Commercial General Liability covers physical bodily injury (slip-and-fall) and tangible property damage. Professional Liability (Errors & Omissions) covers financial losses resulting from consulting advice, software coding, architectural design, or legal services.
What is a Business Owner's Policy (BOP)?
A BOP bundles General Liability, Commercial Property, and Business Interruption insurance into a single cost-effective package for small businesses, typically saving 15% to 25% over separate policies. Read our Business Owner's Policy Guide.

Can an insurance company drop you or cancel your policy after you file a claim?

In most jurisdictions, an insurer cannot immediately cancel your policy mid-term solely because you filed a single legitimate claim, unless there was proven fraud, material misrepresentation, or non-payment.
