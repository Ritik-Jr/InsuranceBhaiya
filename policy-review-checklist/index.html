What This Means

Conducting an annual 10-point policy check protects against passive auto-renewals:

  • Carriers Change Terms at Renewal: Deductibles, wind/hail rules, and roof valuation schedules (switching from replacement cost to ACV) are often modified in renewal notices.
  • Life Changes Demand Policy Updates: Remodeling a kitchen, purchasing fine jewelry, adding a driver, or starting a business can leave recent assets uninsured unless documented.

Frequently Asked Questions: Policy Review Checklist

Expert mathematical answers, formula explanations, and related comparison guides.

What are the 10 critical areas to inspect in an insurance policy?
The 10 essential areas are: Coverage scope, limits, deductibles/excess, exclusions, renewal clauses, cancellation grace periods, claims procedures, beneficiary designations, optional endorsements, and key definitions. Use our interactive Policy Review Checklist.
Where can I find my policy limits and deductible quickly?
On your Declarations Page (often labeled "Dec Page"), typically the first 1–3 pages of your policy packet. Read our Guide to Reading an Insurance Policy.
How often should you conduct a comprehensive insurance policy review?
At least annually 30 days before renewal, and immediately following major life milestones: marriage, purchasing a home, having children, or starting a business.

Can an insurance company drop you or cancel your policy after you file a claim?

In most jurisdictions, an insurer cannot immediately cancel your policy mid-term solely because you filed a single legitimate claim, unless there was proven fraud, material misrepresentation, or non-payment.
