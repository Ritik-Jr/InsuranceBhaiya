Dictionary Entry Auto & Driving UK / Commonwealth: Totting Up (UK term)

Totting Up

Totting up is the UK rule under which a driver who builds up 12 or more penalty points within 3 years is normally disqualified from driving, usually for at least 6 months.

Actuarial & Contractual Mechanics

Points from offences committed within a 3-year window count towards the total. At 12 or more, the court must normally impose a minimum ban: 6 months, rising to 12 months if you've had a ban in the previous 3 years, and 2 years for two or more previous bans. A court can reduce or avoid the ban only if you prove exceptional hardship, which means hardship to others, not just inconvenience to you. New drivers face a stricter 6-point limit in their first 2 years (see 6 points in your first 2 years). Insurers price the underlying convictions (see car insurance with points on your licence) and the disqualification (see car insurance after a driving ban), and many mainstream insurers decline recently banned drivers. Our hub on car insurance for high-risk and convicted drivers covers every route back.

Real-World Dollar Scenario

Mo receives four SP30 speeding convictions of 3 points each within 26 months, reaching 12 points. The court imposes a 6-month totting-up ban. His £680 premium becomes about £1,950 at his first specialist renewal after the ban, then falls to about £1,300 the following year with a telematics policy.

Wallet Risk: What Happens If You Get This Wrong?

A totting-up ban is easy to sleepwalk into through several minor offences. It costs more than the ban itself: years of higher premiums, fewer insurers willing to quote, and convictions you must declare until they're spent (see do you have to declare spent convictions and how long do motoring convictions stay on your licence). Taking a speed awareness course instead of points, when you're offered one, can keep you away from 12 (see tell your insurer about a speeding ticket). Each claim-free year rebuilds your no-claims bonus, which steadily offsets the conviction loading.

After a Totting-Up Ban

Expect specialist pricing at first. Black box insurance for high-risk drivers can speed up the recovery. If mainstream insurers decline you, read can an insurance company refuse to insure you and look at non-standard auto insurance providers. US drivers face state point systems instead, with possible suspensions and SR-22 insurance filings.

Quick Knowledge Check
A driver who passed their test 10 years ago has 9 points and receives 3 more for speeding within the same 3-year window. What normally happens?
They reach 12 points and are normally banned for at least 6 months under totting up.
Correct. 12 or more points within 3 years normally triggers a minimum 6-month disqualification.
• Their licence is revoked and they must retake their test.
Revocation and a retest apply under the New Drivers Act at 6 points within 2 years of passing, not to experienced drivers.
• Nothing happens until they reach 15 points.
The totting-up threshold is 12 points.
• Their insurer must cover them without any increase.
Insurers can increase premiums, or decline, for convictions and bans.
