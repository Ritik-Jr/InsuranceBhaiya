Lay-Up Coverage

Lay-up coverage is a seasonal option that keeps comprehensive protection (theft, fire, vandalism, storm damage) on a stored vehicle, usually a motorcycle, while suspending the riding coverages such as liability and collision until you ride again.

Actuarial & Contractual Mechanics

Think of lay-up coverage as putting your policy into hibernation rather than killing it. During the lay-up period, the coverages that only matter when you ride (liability, collision, and often UM/UIM and medical payments) are suspended, so you stop paying for them. Comprehensive stays active, because a bike sitting in a garage can still be stolen, catch fire or be crushed by a falling shelf. The Insurance Information Institute describes these seasonal policies as mostly offered in northern states with real winters. The big advantage over cancelling is that there's no coverage lapse. A lapse can raise your next premium and leave the stored bike unprotected. See what happens when insurance expires. Lay-up is one of the easiest ways to lower the figures in our average cost of motorcycle insurance guide, and it works alongside the choices in full coverage vs liability-only motorcycle insurance.

Real-World Dollar Scenario

Priya rides her $11,000 cruiser in Ohio from April to October. Her full-coverage policy costs $540 a year, or $45 a month. She puts the bike on lay-up from November through March, five months, with comprehensive only at about $8 a month. The riding coverages she pauses would have cost about $37 a month, so she saves roughly $185 that year (5 × $37). When her garage is broken into in January, her comprehensive claim for the stolen bike is still paid, minus her $500 deductible.

Wallet Risk: What Happens If You Get This Wrong?

Riding a laid-up bike is the danger. If you take it out 'just once' on a warm February day and crash, there's usually no liability or collision coverage in force, which is effectively the same as crashing without insurance. Always reactivate the policy before you ride, and confirm the change in writing. And never cancel outright to save money. A stolen bike with no comprehensive in place is a total loss. Lay-Up Coverage vs Cancelling vs Pausing

Lay-up keeps comprehensive and your continuous-coverage history intact at a lower price. Cancelling saves a little more short-term, but it creates a lapse and leaves the bike unprotected, and some states' registration rules penalize lapses. Pausing is the car-insurance version of the same idea. See can you pause car insurance. Lay-up also matters for financed bikes, because lenders require comprehensive to stay active. If you owe more than the bike is worth, read about gap insurance.

Who Should Use Lay-Up Coverage?

It suits riders in cold-winter states like Ohio, Colorado and the Northeast who store their bike for three months or more, owners of classic or summer-only bikes, and anyone laid up by injury or deployment. Regulatory & Editorial Notice: Not every insurer offers lay-up, and the coverages suspended vary by insurer and state. Confirm the details in writing before storing your bike. Source: Insurance Information Institute.
Quick Knowledge Check
Your motorcycle is on lay-up coverage for the winter. Which of these is still covered?
Your bike is stolen from your locked garage
Correct. Lay-up keeps comprehensive active, and comprehensive covers theft, fire and vandalism while the bike is stored.
• You take the bike for a quick ride on a sunny day and hit a parked car
Not covered. Liability and collision are suspended during lay-up. You must reactivate the riding coverages before you ride.
• Your friend borrows the bike and injures a pedestrian
Not covered. With liability suspended, injuries caused while riding a laid-up bike generally aren't covered.
• All of the above, since the policy is still active
The policy is active, but only comprehensive is in force. That's why lay-up costs less.
