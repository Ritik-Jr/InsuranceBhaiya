Non-Standard Auto Insurance

Non-standard auto insurance is car insurance from insurers that specialize in drivers that mainstream insurers decline or price heavily, such as drivers with DUIs, serious violations, lapses in cover, criminal convictions or unusual vehicles. In the UK, it's usually called specialist or convicted driver insurance.

Actuarial & Contractual Mechanics

Standard insurers accept 'preferred' and 'standard' risk tiers and decline or surcharge the rest. Non-standard carriers build their pricing for those excluded drivers. They typically offer state-minimum or modest liability limits, monthly payment plans, and SR-22 insurance filings. The premium is higher because the expected claims cost is higher. See how much does car insurance go up after a DUI. In the UK, the same niche covers drivers with unspent convictions (see which insurance companies do not ask about criminal convictions) and repaired write-offs (see are Cat N cars more expensive to insure). If even non-standard carriers decline, the US backstop is an assigned risk auto insurance plan. Our hub, car insurance for high-risk and convicted drivers, maps every route.

Real-World Dollar Scenario

After a DUI, Alex's standard insurer non-renewed his $1,600-a-year policy. A non-standard carrier offered state-minimum liability with an SR-22 filing for $2,500 a year, about 56% more, while the cheapest standard insurer still willing to quote charged $3,900. Three years later, with a clean record, Alex re-shopped and returned to a standard carrier at $1,950.

Wallet Risk: What Happens If You Get This Wrong?

Non-standard policies often default to state-minimum limits, which can leave you personally liable for serious accidents (see coverage vs limits). Some also have narrower terms, such as excluded drivers, limited rental cover or stricter cancellation for non-payment. A cancellation during an SR-22 period can suspend your licence. And staying in the non-standard market after your record clears means overpaying. Diarize the date each violation ages off, or each UK conviction becomes spent (see do you have to declare spent convictions and how long do motoring convictions stay on your licence), and re-quote.

Standard vs Non-Standard vs Assigned Risk

Think of three layers. Standard carriers take most drivers. Non-standard carriers voluntarily take higher-risk drivers at higher prices. Assigned risk plans are the state's guaranteed last resort. UK drivers don't have the last layer; specialist brokers fill the gap. Write-off cars sit across all three layers. Compare categories in Cat S vs Cat N car insurance, and see a full example in Ryan's DR10 and Cat N scenario.

Typical non-standard customers include drivers who have been declined elsewhere (see can an insurance company refuse to insure you), drivers returning after a totting up ban or a drink-drive ban (see car insurance after a driving ban), drivers with car insurance with points on your licence, US drivers with felony records (see car insurance with a felony), and drivers using black box insurance for high-risk drivers to rebuild their record.

Quick Knowledge Check
A driver needs an SR-22 after a DUI. Which statement is correct?
Non-standard insurers commonly file SR-22s and insure drivers that standard insurers decline.
Correct. Non-standard carriers specialize in high-risk drivers and routinely file SR-22 certificates with the state.
• Non-standard insurance is the same thing as the state assigned risk plan.
No. Non-standard carriers write voluntarily. The assigned risk plan is the state's last-resort mechanism for drivers no one will write.
• An SR-22 is a type of non-standard insurance policy.
No. An SR-22 is a certificate of financial responsibility attached to a policy, not a policy itself.
• Once in the non-standard market, you can never return to standard rates.
No. As violations age off your record and you keep cover continuous, you can move back to standard carriers.
