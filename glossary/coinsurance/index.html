Dictionary Entry Health & Medical

Coinsurance

The percentage of covered medical expenses you share with your insurer after reaching your deductible.

Actuarial & Contractual Mechanics

After meeting your deductible, coinsurance splits covered expenses as a fixed percentage between you and your insurer (typically 80% paid by insurer, 20% by you) until you reach your out-of-pocket maximum. See how coinsurance alters real hospital invoices with our Out-of-Pocket Cost Calculator and review network rules in HMO vs. PPO.

Real-World Dollar Scenario

After meeting her $2,000 deductible, Maria needs an outpatient knee surgery costing $5,000. Under an 80/20 plan, her insurer pays 80% ($4,000) and Maria pays 20% ($1,000).

Wallet Risk: What Happens If You Get This Wrong?

On large hospital bills, a 20% coinsurance share can easily exceed $15,000 without an annual stop-loss cap. Review policy mechanics in our Health Insurance Basics Guide and check Does Health Insurance Cover Out-of-Network ER Visits?.

Quick Knowledge Check
In an 80/20 coinsurance plan with the deductible already met, how much do you pay on a $1,000 covered procedure?
• $800
$800 is the 80% paid by the insurer.
$200
Bingo! 20% of $1,000 is $200.
• $0
Incorrect, unless your annual out-of-pocket maximum has already been satisfied.

Frequently Asked Questions About Coinsurance

Clear definitions, policy implications, and related coverage calculators.

When does coinsurance kick in during a medical claim?
Coinsurance begins immediately after you satisfy your annual deductible. For example, on an 80/20 plan, the insurer pays 80% and you pay 20% until you reach your Out-of-Pocket Maximum. Simulate total medical costs in our Health Insurance Calculator.
What is the property insurance 80% coinsurance clause?
If you fail to insure your dwelling for at least 80% of its full replacement cost, the insurer will reduce partial claim settlements proportionally. Read our guide on Homeowners Insurance Fundamentals.

Can an insurance company drop you or cancel your policy after you file a claim?

In most jurisdictions, an insurer cannot immediately cancel your policy mid-term solely because you filed a single legitimate claim, unless there was proven fraud, material misrepresentation, or non-payment.
