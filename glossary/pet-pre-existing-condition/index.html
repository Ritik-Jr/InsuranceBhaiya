Pet Pre-Existing Condition

Any illness or injury your pet showed signs of, or was diagnosed with, before the policy began or during its waiting period, which the insurer will usually not cover.

Actuarial & Contractual Mechanics

A pre-existing condition does not need a formal diagnosis; a vet note about limping, scratching or vomiting can be enough. Insurers review medical records, often back 12 months or more, before paying large claims. Curable conditions may be reinstated after a symptom-free period (often 6 to 12 months), but chronic ones usually stay excluded for life, and paired conditions like knee ligaments may be treated as bilateral. Learn how the review works in does pet insurance cover pre-existing conditions, then see how the pet insurance waiting period creates this risk. For the full picture read Pet Insurance Explained.

Real-World Dollar Scenario

Carlos buys a plan for his dog Bruno after a vet note records occasional limping. Five months later Bruno needs a $5,200 cruciate ligament repair. The insurer reads the earlier note, classes the knee problem as pre-existing and denies the claim. Carlos pays $5,200. The same surgery on a healthy puppy with a $500 deductible and 80% reimbursement would have left him paying $1,440.

Wallet Risk: What Happens If You Get This Wrong?

A pre-existing label can remove the exact coverage you bought the policy for, and it can apply to the other side of a paired body part as well. Reduce the risk by enrolling young, keeping policies continuous and asking the insurer in writing how it treats curable and bilateral conditions. Quick Knowledge Check
Your dog was treated for an ear infection 3 months before you bought pet insurance. Six months after enrolling, the ear infection returns. What is the likely outcome?
• Covered, because the infection returned after enrolment
Incorrect. A recurrence of a condition treated before enrolment is generally treated as pre-existing unless the insurer allows reinstatement after a symptom-free period.
Likely excluded unless the policy reinstates curable conditions after a symptom-free period
Correct. Insurers commonly exclude recurrences, and only some allow curable conditions to be covered again after 6 to 12 months without signs or treatment.
• Covered at a reduced reimbursement rate
Incorrect. Pre-existing exclusions are usually all-or-nothing, not reduced payouts.
• Covered only if you pay a higher premium
Incorrect. Premium changes do not remove the pre-existing exclusion.
