What Is Uninsured Boater Coverage?

IB
Insurance Bhaiya Boat & Marine Insurance Group Verified Answer
Reviewed by Licensed Claims Adjuster & Actuarial Auditor • Updated September 2026 • 5 min read
Zero Commission Bias
Quick Answer / Executive Summary

Uninsured boater coverage pays for your injuries and, in some policies, property damage if you're hit by another boater who doesn't carry insurance — the marine equivalent of uninsured motorist coverage on an auto policy. It's an especially important add-on given how many states, as covered in our state-by-state requirements guide, don't legally require other boaters to carry any insurance at all, which means the other party in a collision may have nothing to pay a claim with.

Key Takeaways at a Glance
  • Uninsured boater coverage protects you financially when the at-fault boater in an accident has no insurance of their own to pay for your injuries or damage — conceptually identical to uninsured motorist coverage in auto insurance.
  • Because most US states don't mandate boat insurance at all, a meaningful share of recreational boaters on the water are uninsured, making this coverage more practically important in boating than it might first appear.
  • Some policies also offer underinsured boater coverage, which fills the gap when the at-fault boater has some insurance, but not enough to fully cover your claim.
  • Coverage typically focuses primarily on bodily injury, though some policies extend it to property damage to your boat as well — this varies by insurer and should be confirmed specifically.
  • This coverage is usually a relatively low-cost add-on to a standard boat policy, given how directly it addresses a documented, common real-world gap in the boating insurance landscape.

Why does this matter so much more in boating than it might in driving?

Unlike auto insurance, where nearly every state legally requires liability coverage, boat insurance is voluntary in most states, with only a small number of exceptions like Arkansas mandating coverage, and generally only above a specific horsepower threshold. That means a real, meaningful share of boaters on the water at any given time may be carrying no insurance at all, so if one of them causes an accident that injures you or damages your boat, there may be no insurance policy on their side to pay for it — exactly the gap uninsured boater coverage exists to close.

What's the difference between uninsured and underinsured boater coverage?

Uninsured boater coverage responds when the at-fault boater has no insurance whatsoever. Underinsured boater coverage, sometimes bundled with it or offered as a separate option, responds when the at-fault boater does have some insurance, but their liability limits are too low to fully cover your claim — for example, a serious injury claim that exceeds the other boater's modest liability limit. Together, these two coverages address the two most common gaps left by relying entirely on the other party's insurance.

What does it actually pay for?

Most uninsured boater coverage is centered on bodily injury — your own medical expenses, lost income, and related costs if you're injured by an uninsured boater. Some, but not all, policies extend this coverage to property damage as well, covering repair costs to your own boat when an uninsured boater is at fault. Because this varies by insurer, it's worth confirming specifically whether your policy's uninsured boater coverage includes property damage or is limited to bodily injury only.
Real-Life Case Incident & Precedent
Precedent: Many marine insurers offer uninsured boater coverage specifically because state boat insurance mandates are the exception rather than the rule nationally, creating a documented, common coverage gap this add-on is designed to address.

Case Study: A Collision With an Uninsured Boater on an Inland Lake

Scenario: A boater on a Midwestern lake, in a state with no boat insurance mandate, is struck by another vessel operated by a boater who carries no insurance at all. The injured boater requires medical treatment and the boat sustains meaningful hull damage.

Resolution & Judicial Outcome: Because the injured boater's own policy included uninsured boater coverage with both bodily injury and property damage components, their insurer pays for the medical expenses and hull repair directly, then separately pursues the at-fault boater for reimbursement. Without this coverage, the injured boater would have had to pursue the uninsured boater personally through a lawsuit, with no guarantee of ever collecting a judgment.

What You Should Do: Step-by-Step Action Plan

1 Step 1: Ask your marine insurer directly whether uninsured and underinsured boater coverage are included in your policy or available as an add-on.
2 Step 2: Confirm whether the coverage includes property damage to your own boat, or is limited to bodily injury only.
3 Step 3: Check the specific coverage limit offered, since it should reasonably match your own liability limits rather than defaulting to a minimal amount.
4 Step 4: If you frequently boat in a state with no insurance mandate, treat this coverage as a higher priority given the realistic chance of encountering an uninsured boater.
5 Step 5: Document any on-water incident thoroughly (photos, witness contact information, a copy of the other boater's registration) in case you need to pursue an uninsured boater claim.

Critical Mistakes to Avoid

  • Assuming every boater on the water carries insurance, when most states don't require it at all.
  • Skipping uninsured boater coverage because it seems like an unnecessary add-on, without checking how common uninsured boating actually is in your state.
  • Not confirming whether your specific policy's uninsured boater coverage extends to property damage, and being surprised later if it only covers injuries.
  • Failing to gather the other boater's information after an accident, which can complicate pursuing an uninsured boater claim later.
  • Treating this page in isolation — this is one of several topics covered in our full guide, Boat Insurance by State: Requirements & Costs.

Can you cancel an insurance policy at any time and get a prorated refund?

Yes, you have the legal right to cancel an insurance policy at any point during its term. You are entitled to a refund of any unused, prepaid premiums. Most personal auto, home, and life policies calculate refunds on a 100% pro-rata basis, though some carriers apply a modest 'short-rate' cancellation penalty (usually around 10% of unearned premium) if you cancel early.

Is Puffin Travel Insurance Legit and Worth Buying?

Yes, Puffin Travel Insurance is a legitimate, UK-regulated travel insurer. It's a trading name of Puffin Group UK Ltd, authorised and regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority, with policies underwritten by Inter Partner Assistance S.A., part of the AXA Group. Independent reviews generally rate it good value, with strong medical and gadget cover limits, though its cancellation cover cap and closed list of covered cancellation reasons are worth checking before you buy. Is Insure 90 a Legitimate Insurance Company?

"Insure 90" is not one identifiable, licensed insurance company — the term is a mix of at least three unrelated things that happen to share similar wording: a legacy insurance-agency software system (I/90, sometimes called Insure90), the insurance concept of a 90% coinsurance requirement, and various small, unrelated quote or agency sites using '90' in their name or marketing. There is no single national carrier operating under this exact brand, so it can't be verified as legitimate or illegitimate as a company — you need to identify the specific business behind whatever quote or policy you actually received. It's one of the clearest cases covered in our [guide to vetting any insurance company](/learn/insurance-company-reviews-is-brand-legit).
