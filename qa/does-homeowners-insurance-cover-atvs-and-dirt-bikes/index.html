Does homeowners insurance cover ATVs and dirt bikes?

IB
Insurance Bhaiya Home Claims Group Verified Answer
Reviewed by Licensed Property & Casualty Coverage Analyst • Updated September 2026 • 6 min read
Zero Commission Bias
Quick Answer / Executive Summary

Usually not, or only in very narrow cases. Standard homeowners policies contain a motorized land vehicle exclusion. It generally removes coverage for the ATV or dirt bike itself (theft, fire, damage) and for injuries it causes away from your property. Some policies give limited liability for recreational vehicles used on the insured premises, and some insurers sell an endorsement to extend it. For real protection, most riders need a separate ATV or dirt bike policy.

Key Takeaways at a Glance
  • Homeowners policies are written for your home and belongings. Motorized vehicles are carved out by an exclusion, with narrow exceptions.
  • Theft of the ATV or dirt bike itself, even from your locked garage, is commonly excluded from personal property coverage.
  • Liability for injuries caused by an ATV or dirt bike off your property is almost always excluded. On-premises liability depends on your exact policy form.
  • A recreational vehicle endorsement may extend liability, but it rarely covers physical damage to the machine.
  • A standalone off-road policy with liability plus comprehensive is often inexpensive and closes both gaps. See do dirt bikes need insurance.

How the Motorized Vehicle Exclusion Works

Almost every standard homeowners form, including the common HO-3, draws a line around motor vehicles. In plain English, it usually does two things:

1. Property coverage. 'Motor vehicles' are removed from covered personal property, apart from narrow exceptions such as vehicles used to service the home (a riding mower, for example) or mobility devices. A quad or dirt bike stored in your garage generally doesn't qualify.
2. Liability coverage. Bodily injury and property damage arising from owning or using a motor vehicle are excluded. Some forms carve back limited coverage for recreational vehicles that aren't registered for road use while on an insured location, and the details vary by form and insurer.

That's why 'does homeowners insurance cover ATVs' so often gets the answer 'it depends, and mostly no'. To read your own policy, look up the definitions of 'motor vehicle' and 'insured location', then the exclusions section. How to read an insurance policy walks you through it, and the wider picture is in home insurance fundamentals.
Why Insurers Exclude Them

Off-road machines carry high injury and theft risk. The CPSC reports an annual average of more than 800 deaths and about 100,000 ER-treated injuries involving off-highway vehicles. Insurers price that risk in dedicated powersport policies instead of spreading it across every homeowner.

Does Homeowners Insurance Cover a Dirt Bike or ATV That's Stolen?

In most cases, no. Because the machine is carved out of personal property coverage, a stolen ATV or dirt bike usually isn't paid under your homeowners policy, even if thieves broke into your garage to take it. Tools and riding gear stolen at the same time may be covered as personal property, minus your deductible. The machine itself needs comprehensive on a powersport policy, which works the same way as motorcycle theft coverage. Pedal bicycles are different: they're normally covered as personal property. See bicycle insurance.

What If Someone Gets Hurt on My ATV or Dirt Bike?

This is the scenario that bankrupts families, so it's worth being precise:

• A guest is hurt on your property: Coverage depends on your policy form. Some policies give limited on-premises liability for unregistered recreational vehicles, and many exclude it. Don't assume either way. Ask your insurer in writing.
• A guest is hurt on a trail, at a friend's farm or at an OHV park: Almost always excluded under homeowners.
• A minor borrows the machine: Some policies exclude or limit coverage for vehicles operated by underage riders.

If you're sued and your policy doesn't respond, you pay for your own lawyer and any judgment. Read can an insurance company sue you for an accident. An umbrella policy usually requires underlying coverage for the vehicle before it will respond.
Lending Is the Riskiest Moment

Most off-road injury lawsuits start with 'we let the neighbor's kid have a go'. If your homeowners policy excludes the ATV and you have no off-road policy, you're effectively self-insured for a claim that can reach six figures.

Endorsement or Standalone Policy? How to Close the Gap

Option 1: Homeowners endorsement. Some insurers sell an endorsement that extends liability to specified recreational vehicles, sometimes off-premises too. It's cheap and convenient, but it usually doesn't cover damage to or theft of the machine. See what is an insurance endorsement.
Option 2: Standalone ATV or dirt bike policy. This includes liability, comprehensive (theft and fire), optional collision, medical payments and guest-rider cover. It's usually the better choice for anything worth more than a few thousand dollars, or anything ridden away from home. Details are in quad bike & ATV insurance and dirt bike insurance coverage.
Option 3: Farm or ranch policy for working quads, with the machine scheduled by VIN.

Some states require liability insurance when you ride off your own land or on public trails. Pennsylvania, for example, requires it for ATVs ridden on land you don't own or lease (PA DCNR). Check the rules in do dirt bikes need insurance.

Real-Life Case Incident & Precedent
Precedent: Homeowners motorized land vehicle exclusion (personal property and liability); personal property coverage for non-motorized contents

Illustrative Case: The Garage Break-In That Homeowners Wouldn't Pay

Scenario: The Novak family's $9,500 sport quad and a $4,200 youth dirt bike were stolen from their locked detached garage, along with $1,800 of riding gear and tools. They filed a homeowners claim with a $1,000 deductible, expecting to recover about $14,500.

Resolution & Judicial Outcome: The insurer paid $800 for the gear and tools ($1,800 minus the $1,000 deductible) and denied the $13,700 claim for the two machines under the motorized land vehicle exclusion. Standalone off-road policies with comprehensive on both would have covered the machines at actual cash value.

What You Should Do: Step-by-Step Action Plan

1 Read the 'motor vehicle' definition and exclusions in your homeowners policy, or ask your insurer to confirm coverage for your ATV or dirt bike in writing.
2 List every off-road machine you own, with VINs, values and where each is ridden.
3 Buy a standalone off-road policy (liability plus comprehensive) for anything valuable or ridden away from home.
4 If you rely on an endorsement, confirm whether it covers off-premises use and underage riders.
5 Check your full insurance picture for gaps with the coverage gap checker.

Critical Mistakes to Avoid

  • Assuming 'it's in my garage, so homeowners covers it'.
  • Letting friends or neighbors' kids ride without confirming liability coverage.
  • Buying an umbrella policy without the underlying vehicle coverage it requires.
  • Treating an OHV registration sticker as proof of insurance.

Does homeowners insurance cover water damage from burst pipes, leaks, or flooding?

Homeowners insurance covers water damage only if it is sudden, accidental, and internal—such as a burst pipe, water heater rupture, or sudden dishwasher hose failure. It strictly excludes flood damage from rising outdoor water, storm surges, sewer or drain backups (unless you purchased a specific endorsement), and gradual damage from unresolved leaks or poor maintenance.

How much does insurance pay out on a claim: Actual Cash Value or Replacement Cost?

How much your insurance pays depends on your policy's valuation clause. Replacement Cost Value (RCV) pays the full current retail cost to replace damaged property with new materials of like kind and quality, with zero deduction for depreciation. Actual Cash Value (ACV) pays only the depreciated fair market value (Replacement Cost minus age, wear, and tear), resulting in dramatically smaller claim payouts.

Is Slide Insurance going out of business

No — based on the most recent public reporting, Slide Insurance is not going out of business. It's a Tampa-based homeowners insurer founded in 2021 that has grown by acquiring renewal rights to large blocks of Florida policies from carriers exiting the state, including UPC Insurance (which became insolvent) and Farmers (which cited Florida's risk exposure). AM Best has not yet rated Slide, but Demotech, a ratings agency widely accepted in Florida's property insurance market, rates it "A" (Exceptional).
