Is Bamboo Insurance Admitted in California?

By Insurance Bhaiya Home Insurance Group • Reviewed by Licensed Claims Adjuster & Actuarial Auditor
Quick Answer / Executive Summary

It depends on the specific policy. Bamboo Insurance is a managing general agent (MGA), not an insurance company itself, and it places California homeowners coverage through several different underwriting carriers — some admitted, some surplus lines (non-admitted). Since 2025 it has expanded admitted capacity through partners like Sutton National and MS Transverse, alongside an E&S 'Signature' surplus-lines product. Always check the underwriting carrier's name on your own declarations page rather than assuming Bamboo's status covers your specific policy. For the broader method behind this check, see our guide to vetting any insurance company.

Key Takeaways at a Glance
  • Bamboo is a managing general agent (MGA), meaning it underwrites and administers policies using delegated authority from partner carriers — it does not carry the insurance risk on its own balance sheet.
  • Bamboo's California homeowners book is split across admitted carriers and surplus lines (non-admitted) carriers, and the mix has been shifting toward more admitted capacity through 2025–2026.
  • A July 2026 partnership with MS Transverse Insurance Company added roughly $150 million in admitted homeowners and dwelling-fire capacity in constrained regions like Los Angeles, San Diego, and San Francisco.
  • Admitted coverage participates in California's guaranty fund protections and standard rate regulation; surplus lines coverage does not, which matters if the underlying carrier were ever to fail.
  • Regulatory filings show Bamboo Ide8 Insurance Services holds a California MGA license and separate Texas agency licenses, confirming it operates as a licensed intermediary rather than an unlicensed operation.

What kind of company Bamboo actually is

Bamboo Insurance is a technology-driven managing general agency headquartered in Midvale, Utah, founded in 2018. As an MGA, it builds proprietary underwriting models for wildfire-exposed homes, prices and issues policies, and handles much of the day-to-day servicing — but every policy is ultimately backed by a licensed insurance carrier partner, not by Bamboo itself. CVC Capital Partners took a majority ownership stake in the business in late 2025, with White Mountains Insurance Group retaining a minority interest, giving Bamboo additional capital to keep expanding its underwriting platform into new states.

Admitted vs. surplus lines: how Bamboo's California book is actually structured

Most of Bamboo's California homeowners policies have historically been underwritten by Sutton National Insurance Company, an admitted California carrier, with a smaller share placed through surplus-lines markets. Since 2025, Bamboo has also offered an excess and surplus 'Signature' product backed by Accredited to widen its appetite for harder-to-place homes that admitted carriers decline. In July 2026, Bamboo announced a new partnership with MS Transverse Insurance Company adding approximately $150 million in admitted homeowners and dwelling-fire capacity, specifically targeting constrained regions such as Los Angeles, San Diego, and San Francisco — a direct response to California's Sustainable Insurance Strategy reforms, which are designed to encourage admitted carriers back into wildfire-distressed markets.

Why the admitted-vs-surplus distinction matters for you

If your specific Bamboo-placed policy is written by an admitted carrier, it's subject to standard California rate regulation and backed by the state's insurance guaranty association if the carrier becomes insolvent. If it's placed through a surplus lines carrier, it isn't backed by that guaranty fund and isn't subject to the same rate caps — which is often exactly why a surplus lines policy was needed in the first place, for a high-wildfire-risk home an admitted carrier wouldn't take on. Neither option is inherently a red flag, but consumer advocates have noted that MGAs working across both admitted and non-admitted carriers can make it harder for policyholders to know who is ultimately responsible when a claim is delayed or denied, which is exactly why checking your specific underwriting carrier matters. Slide Insurance's own move into California as a direct, non-admitted surplus lines carrier is a useful point of comparison for how this same admitted-versus-surplus distinction plays out for a full-fledged insurer rather than an MGA.
Real-Life Case Incident & Precedent
Precedent: California Insurance Code distinguishes admitted insurers, who must be licensed and participate in guaranty associations, from non-admitted surplus lines insurers, who operate under separate placement rules requiring a diligent search of the admitted market first.

Case Study: A Los Angeles Homeowner Comparing FAIR Plan and Bamboo

Scenario: A homeowner in a high-wildfire-risk Los Angeles ZIP code was non-renewed by a national admitted carrier and is comparing a California FAIR Plan policy against a quote sourced through an independent agent using Bamboo.

Resolution & Judicial Outcome: The agent's Bamboo-sourced quote comes back underwritten by Sutton National Insurance Company, an admitted carrier, rather than through the surplus-lines Signature product. Because it's admitted, the homeowner confirms it's backed by the California Insurance Guarantee Association and subject to standard rate review, and the total cost comes in below the combination of a FAIR Plan policy plus a separate difference-in-conditions (DIC) wrap policy that would otherwise be needed for full coverage.

What You Should Do: Step-by-Step Action Plan

1 Step 1: Ask your independent agent directly which underwriting carrier your Bamboo-placed quote comes from — Sutton National, MS Transverse, Accredited, or another partner — rather than assuming.
2 Step 2: Look up that specific carrier's admitted status on the California Department of Insurance's company search.
3 Step 3: If the carrier is non-admitted (surplus lines), confirm your agent completed the required diligent search of the admitted market and provided the surplus lines disclosure notice.
4 Step 4: Check the underwriting carrier's AM Best financial strength rating, since that — not Bamboo's own rating as an MGA — determines who actually pays your claim.
5 Step 5: Keep a copy of your declarations page showing the underwriting carrier's exact legal name for future reference if you ever need to file a complaint or claim dispute, following the same five-step legitimacy check used for any other insurer.

Critical Mistakes to Avoid

  • Assuming 'Bamboo Insurance' itself is licensed as a carrier in California — it is a managing general agent, and the licensing status that matters is the underlying carrier's.
  • Treating a July 2026 announcement about new admitted capacity as if it automatically applies to every Bamboo policy, including ones written before that partnership or through the surplus-lines Signature product.
  • Skipping the surplus lines disclosure paperwork, which explains in writing that a non-admitted policy isn't backed by the state guaranty fund.
  • Comparing only the premium between a FAIR Plan policy and a Bamboo-sourced quote without also comparing what coverage and financial backing each option actually provides.

Does homeowners insurance cover water damage from burst pipes, leaks, or flooding?

Homeowners insurance covers water damage only if it is sudden, accidental, and internal—such as a burst pipe, water heater rupture, or sudden dishwasher hose failure. It strictly excludes flood damage from rising outdoor water, storm surges, sewer or drain backups (unless you purchased a specific endorsement), and gradual damage from unresolved leaks or poor maintenance.

How much does insurance pay out on a claim: Actual Cash Value or Replacement Cost?

How much your insurance pays depends on your policy's valuation clause. Replacement Cost Value (RCV) pays the full current retail cost to replace damaged property with new materials of like kind and quality, with zero deduction for depreciation. Actual Cash Value (ACV) pays only the depreciated fair market value (Replacement Cost minus age, wear, and tear), resulting in dramatically smaller claim payouts.

Can an insurance company drop you or cancel your policy after you file a claim?

In most jurisdictions, an insurer cannot immediately cancel your policy mid-term solely because you filed a single legitimate claim, unless there was proven fraud, material misrepresentation, or non-payment. However, they are legally permitted to non-renew your policy when the term expires (typically every 6 or 12 months) by providing advance written notice.
