How much is motorcycle insurance in Colorado?

IB
Insurance Bhaiya Auto Claims Group Verified Answer
Reviewed by Licensed Property & Casualty Rate Analyst • Updated September 2026 • 6 min read
Zero Commission Bias
Quick Answer / Executive Summary

Motorcycle insurance in Colorado averages roughly $117 a year (about $10 a month) for minimum liability and about $567 a year (about $47 a month) for full coverage, according to MoneyGeek's 2026 rate analysis. Colorado's legal minimum is 25/50/15 liability. Your real price depends mostly on your bike type, age, riding record and ZIP code. Young riders on sport bikes can pay several times the average.

Key Takeaways at a Glance
  • Colorado requires motorcycle liability of at least $25,000 per person, $50,000 per accident and $15,000 property damage, per the Colorado Division of Insurance.
  • Estimated averages are about $117 a year for minimum coverage and about $567 a year for full coverage. Colorado ranks around 17th cheapest for minimum and 24th for full coverage nationally.
  • A $15,000 property damage minimum is low for Colorado roads full of late-model trucks and SUVs. One crash can exceed it.
  • Denver-metro ZIP codes usually pay more for comprehensive (theft and hail) than rural Western Slope ZIP codes.
  • Off-highway riding on public land needs a Colorado Parks & Wildlife OHV registration or permit, not auto insurance. See do dirt bikes need insurance.

Minimum liability (25/50/15): about $117/year (about $10/month)
Full coverage (liability + collision + comprehensive): about $567/year (about $47/month)

Source: MoneyGeek Colorado motorcycle insurance analysis. In the same dataset, the lowest median full-coverage quotes came from Shelter (about $199/year), Markel (about $282), Dairyland (about $323), GEICO (about $340) and Progressive (about $341). For comparison, see how much is motorcycle insurance in Ohio. Both states sit near the national middle, but Ohio's higher property damage minimum changes the picture.
Why Averages Mislead

An 'average' blends 55-year-old cruiser riders paying $80 a year with 20-year-old supersport riders paying $2,000 or more. Treat averages as a starting point, then quote your own profile. The premium calculator helps you see how each factor moves the number.

Colorado Motorcycle Insurance Requirements

Colorado applies the same compulsory liability law to motorcycles as to cars:

• Bodily injury: $25,000 per person / $50,000 per accident
• Property damage: $15,000 per accident
• Proof of insurance is required to register the bike and must be carried while riding.

Insurers also routinely offer uninsured/underinsured motorist (UM/UIM) and medical payments coverage. Decline them only after careful thought. Motorcyclists are far more likely than drivers to be injured in a crash, and NHTSA data shows that in 2024, per mile traveled, riders were almost 27 times more likely than car occupants to die in a crash and almost 5 times more likely to be injured. Colorado's helmet law applies to riders and passengers under 18, and eye protection rules apply more broadly. The IIHS helmet-law map tracks current rules.

Riding without coverage in Colorado can mean fines, license suspension and personal liability for every dollar of damage. See is it against the law to drive without insurance and what happens if you crash without insurance.
The $15,000 Property Damage Problem

Clip a new pickup or two parked cars and $15,000 is gone quickly. Anything above it comes out of your savings. Most riders with assets should carry at least $50,000 property damage, and consider an umbrella policy once their net worth exceeds their liability limits. Check yours with how to know if you are underinsured.

What Drives Colorado Motorcycle Insurance Rates Up or Down?

1. Bike type and engine size. Supersport bikes cost the most. Cruisers, touring bikes and standards cost the least.
2. Rider age and license history. Riders under 25, newly endorsed riders and permit riders pay the most. Read can you get motorcycle insurance without a license.
3. ZIP code. Denver, Aurora and Colorado Springs see more theft and traffic, which raises comprehensive and liability. Hail exposure along the Front Range affects comprehensive too. For how theft claims are valued, see does motorcycle insurance cover theft.
4. Riding season. Mountain winters mean many riders store bikes for months. A lay-up option can cut costs without a coverage lapse. See can you pause car insurance.
5. Deductibles. Raising collision and comprehensive deductibles from $250 to $1,000 lowers the premium. Check the break-even with the car insurance deductible calculator.
6. Record and discounts. A clean record, a rider course, bundling and paying in full all help. A single at-fault crash can raise rates for years. See how long does an accident stay on your insurance.

Colorado Off-Road Riders: Dirt Bikes, ATVs and OHV Rules

Colorado's trail system is a big draw for dirt bike and ATV riders. Off-highway vehicles ridden on public land need a Colorado Parks & Wildlife OHV registration or permit, which is separate from insurance. The state doesn't mandate liability insurance for trail-only OHVs, but a crash on a shared trail can still lead to a lawsuit. Learn what off-road policies cover in dirt bike insurance coverage and quad bike & ATV insurance. The full two-wheel and powersport picture is in our motorcycle and powersport insurance hub.

Real-Life Case Incident & Precedent
Precedent: Colorado compulsory liability minimums (25/50/15); motorcycle rating by engine class, age, experience and territory

Illustrative Case: Two Denver Riders, One $1,700 Price Gap

Scenario: Anna, 42, rides a 900cc cruiser, has 15 years of licensed riding and a clean record, and stores the bike from November to March. Jake, 22, rides a 1,000cc supersport, was endorsed last year and parks on the street in central Denver. Both asked for full coverage with 50/100/50 liability and a $500 deductible.

Resolution & Judicial Outcome: Anna's quotes came in around $400 a year with a lay-up discount. Jake's came in above $2,000. By switching to a 650cc standard, taking a rider course and raising his deductible to $1,000, Jake brought his quotes down substantially while keeping the same liability limits.

What You Should Do: Step-by-Step Action Plan

1 Start with Colorado's 25/50/15 minimum, then decide how much higher to go based on your savings. Many riders choose 50/100/50 or more.
2 Add UM/UIM and medical payments unless your health and disability coverage is truly robust.
3 Get at least three quotes for the same limits and deductibles so you're comparing like with like.
4 Ask about a winter lay-up option and rider-course, bundling and paid-in-full discounts.
5 Register trail-only dirt bikes and ATVs with Colorado Parks & Wildlife and decide separately whether to buy off-road liability.

Critical Mistakes to Avoid

  • Treating the $117/year average as your quote. Your profile may price far higher or lower.
  • Stopping at $15,000 property damage when one crash with a new truck can cost more.
  • Cancelling coverage in winter instead of using a lay-up option, which creates a lapse and costs more later.
  • Assuming your OHV registration sticker is insurance. It isn't.

Can an insurance company drop you or cancel your policy after you file a claim?

In most jurisdictions, an insurer cannot immediately cancel your policy mid-term solely because you filed a single legitimate claim, unless there was proven fraud, material misrepresentation, or non-payment. However, they are legally permitted to non-renew your policy when the term expires (typically every 6 or 12 months) by providing advance written notice.

What happens legally and financially if you get into a car crash without insurance?

Driving without insurance and causing an accident results in catastrophic personal financial liability and severe legal penalties. You are personally liable for 100% of all medical bills, property damages, and legal judgments out of pocket. Legally, authorities can suspend your driver's license, impound your vehicle, impose thousands in fines, and mandate SR-22 high-risk insurance for years.

Why did my car insurance premium increase even though I had no accidents or tickets?

Car insurance rates can increase substantially without any tickets or accidents due to macroeconomic rate revisions, rising vehicle repair costs, increased frequency of catastrophic weather losses in your zip code, insurance credit score changes, and widespread insurance industry underwriting adjustments across your state.

Does your personal auto insurance cover rental cars, or do you need rental counter coverage?

In the United States and Canada, your personal auto insurance coverage (comprehensive, collision, and liability) typically transfers directly to a rental car driven for personal pleasure, up to your existing policy limits and deductibles. However, personal policies almost never cover 'loss of use' administrative fees charged by rental companies, and do not provide coverage internationally.
