Can you get car insurance with points on your licence, and how much more will it cost?

IB
Insurance Bhaiya Auto Claims Group Verified Answer
Reviewed by Licensed Motor Underwriter • Updated October 2026 • 5 min read
Zero Commission Bias
Quick Answer / Executive Summary

Yes. Most UK insurers will still cover you with penalty points, but you'll pay more. Admiral's October 2023 quote data showed an SP30 speeding conviction with 1–3 points raising premiums by about 34%, 4–6 points by about 58%, and 7+ points by about 94%. A CU80 mobile-phone conviction cost more, at roughly +55% for 1–3 points and +174% for 7+. You must declare unspent motoring convictions when asked, usually for 5 years. Points on their own expire sooner. See how long do motoring convictions stay on your licence and our hub on car insurance for high-risk and convicted drivers.

Key Takeaways at a Glance
  • Points rarely stop you getting cover, but they raise the premium, and the offence code matters as much as the number of points.
  • Indicative uplifts (Admiral, Oct 2023): SP30 +34% (1–3 pts), +58% (4–6), +94% (7+). CU80 +55% (1–3 pts), +71% (4–6), +174% (7+).
  • At 12 points within 3 years, totting up normally means a ban of at least 6 months. After that, see car insurance after a driving ban.
  • New drivers lose their licence at just 6 points in the first 2 years. See 6 points in your first 2 years.
  • Declare points while the conviction is unspent, typically 5 years from conviction for adults (see do you have to declare spent convictions).

How Insurers Price Penalty Points

Insurers price the offence code, the number of points and how recent the conviction is. Speeding (SP30) is common and moderately priced. Mobile-phone use (CU80), careless driving and no insurance (IN10 conviction) are priced more heavily, and drink driving (DR10) roughly doubles a premium at first. The loading falls each year as the conviction ages, which is also how underwriting treats US violations. See how long does reckless driving affect insurance and how much does car insurance go up after a DUI.

What You Must Declare

Answer the insurer's questions accurately, as the Consumer Insurance (Disclosure and Representations) Act 2012 requires. Most forms ask about motoring convictions in the last 5 years. Once a conviction is spent, you don't have to declare it. A speed awareness course isn't a conviction and carries no points. Whether you need to mention it is covered in tell your insurer about a speeding ticket. Named drivers' points count too. See named driver with a conviction.
Hiding Points Doesn't Work

Insurers can check your licence record through the DVLA's MyLicence service. A false answer can void the policy and lead to refused claims. If it's deliberate, it's fraud. See which insurance companies do not ask about criminal convictions for what you lawfully don't have to say.

How to Keep the Premium Down With Points

1. Compare widely: loadings for the same code vary a lot between insurers. Start with best rate on car insurance.
2. Consider telematics: see black box insurance for high-risk drivers.
3. Adjust your voluntary excess using the Car Insurance Deductible Calculator.
4. Protect your no-claims bonus, which offsets the conviction loading over time.
5. Re-quote when the points expire and again when the conviction becomes spent.

If a mainstream insurer declines you, read can an insurance company refuse to insure you, then try non-standard auto insurance specialists.
Real-Life Case Incident & Precedent
Precedent: Road Traffic Offenders Act 1988 (penalty points); Admiral quote analysis, October 2023.

Illustrative Case: Two Speeding Tickets in 18 Months

Scenario: Aisha, 31, paid £520 a year with a clean licence. She then received two SP30 convictions (3 points each) 18 months apart, for 6 points in total.

Resolution & Judicial Outcome: After the first conviction, her renewal rose to about £700 (+34%). After the second, the cheapest quote was about £820 (+58%). Three more points within the 3-year window would take her to 9, and four more to 12, the totting up threshold. She took a telematics policy and diarized the dates when each conviction would become spent.

What You Should Do: Step-by-Step Action Plan

1 Check your licence on GOV.UK for codes, points and dates.
2 Note when each set of points stops counting for totting up (3 years) and when each conviction becomes spent.
3 Get quotes from mainstream insurers and specialists, declaring every unspent conviction.
4 Consider telematics and a sensible voluntary excess.
5 Re-quote at each expiry date.

Critical Mistakes to Avoid

  • Leaving points off the form. Insurers can check your licence record.
  • Accepting an automatic renewal after a new conviction without comparing.
  • Forgetting that 6 points as a new driver means revocation.

Can an insurance company drop you or cancel your policy after you file a claim?

In most jurisdictions, an insurer cannot immediately cancel your policy mid-term solely because you filed a single legitimate claim, unless there was proven fraud, material misrepresentation, or non-payment. However, they are legally permitted to non-renew your policy when the term expires (typically every 6 or 12 months) by providing advance written notice.

What happens legally and financially if you get into a car crash without insurance?

Driving without insurance and causing an accident results in catastrophic personal financial liability and severe legal penalties. You are personally liable for 100% of all medical bills, property damages, and legal judgments out of pocket. Legally, authorities can suspend your driver's license, impound your vehicle, impose thousands in fines, and mandate SR-22 high-risk insurance for years.

Why did my car insurance premium increase even though I had no accidents or tickets?

Car insurance rates can increase substantially without any tickets or accidents due to macroeconomic rate revisions, rising vehicle repair costs, increased frequency of catastrophic weather losses in your zip code, insurance credit score changes, and widespread insurance industry underwriting adjustments across your state.

Does your personal auto insurance cover rental cars, or do you need rental counter coverage?

In the United States and Canada, your personal auto insurance coverage (comprehensive, collision, and liability) typically transfers directly to a rental car driven for personal pleasure, up to your existing policy limits and deductibles. However, personal policies almost never cover 'loss of use' administrative fees charged by rental companies, and do not provide coverage internationally.
