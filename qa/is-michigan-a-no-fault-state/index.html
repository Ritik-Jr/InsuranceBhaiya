Is Michigan a no-fault state?

IB
Insurance Bhaiya Editorial Team
Updated October 2026 • 7 min read
Quick Answer / Executive Summary

Yes. Michigan is a no-fault state, and its system is one of the most distinctive in the country. Your own personal injury protection (PIP) pays your medical bills and lost wages after a crash, whoever caused it. Since Michigan's 2019 reform took effect for policies issued or renewed after July 1, 2020, you can choose your PIP medical level, from unlimited down to $250,000 or lower, and some people on Medicare can opt out entirely. The lower the PIP you choose, the more you rely on health insurance and on suing the at-fault driver for medical costs above your limit.

Key Takeaways at a Glance
  • Michigan is a no-fault state. Your own PIP pays first, regardless of fault. See the full list in no-fault vs at-fault car insurance states.
  • There are six PIP medical choices: unlimited, $500,000, $250,000, $250,000 with exclusions, $50,000 (Medicaid only) and no PIP medical (Medicare Parts A and B only) (Michigan DIFS).
  • Choosing less than unlimited lowers your premium but can leave you exposed after a catastrophic injury. Medical bills above your PIP limit may be claimed from the at-fault driver.
  • Michigan uses a verbal tort threshold. You can sue for pain and suffering only for death, serious impairment of body function or permanent serious disfigurement.
  • Vehicle damage works differently too. Michigan's 'mini-tort' lets you recover up to $3,000 from the at-fault driver for repair costs your insurance doesn't pay.

How Michigan's No-Fault System Works

Michigan adopted no-fault in 1973, and for decades it was the only state requiring unlimited lifetime medical PIP. That made it excellent for catastrophic injuries and very expensive for drivers. The 2019 reform (Public Act 21) kept the no-fault structure but added choice:

• Your own PIP pays first for medical bills, lost wages (generally 85% of gross income, up to a monthly cap, for up to three years), replacement services and attendant care, regardless of fault.
• You can sue the at-fault driver for pain and suffering only if your injury meets Michigan's verbal threshold, and for economic losses above your PIP benefits.
• Vehicle damage is handled through your own collision coverage plus the mini-tort claim of up to $3,000.

For how PIP works everywhere, see personal injury protection (PIP). For how Michigan compares with New York's $50,000 system, see New York no-fault insurance. To understand how lawsuit thresholds differ in states offering election rights, compare full tort vs limited tort options.

Michigan PIP Choices Explained

According to the Michigan Department of Insurance and Financial Services, drivers can choose:

1. Unlimited lifetime medical coverage
2. $500,000 per person per accident
3. $250,000 per person per accident
4. $250,000 with exclusions, where household members with qualified health coverage are excluded
5. $50,000, available only if you're enrolled in Medicaid and your household members have qualifying coverage
6. No PIP medical coverage, available only if you have Medicare Parts A and B and your household members have qualifying coverage

Unlimited PIP also carries the full Michigan Catastrophic Claims Association (MCCA) assessment. Lower levels pay only the deficit portion, which is part of why they're cheaper.
The Catastrophic Injury Trap

Severe brain or spinal injuries can cost millions over a lifetime in attendant care, rehab and home modifications. Health insurance often limits long-term attendant care, and a lawsuit against an at-fault driver who carries modest liability limits may not cover the difference. Before you drop from unlimited, check what your health plan really covers using the health insurance calculator.

Michigan Liability Limits Changed Too

Because people with lower PIP can now sue for excess medical costs, the reform raised default bodily injury liability to $250,000 per person and $500,000 per accident. You can sign a form to choose lower limits, down to a $50,000/$100,000 minimum. Lowering them saves money but increases your personal exposure if you injure someone who chose low PIP. Most drivers with assets should keep at least the defaults and consider an umbrella policy. Check your exposure with how to know if you are underinsured.
Michigan Has a High Uninsured Rate

Michigan has historically had one of the highest shares of uninsured drivers in the country (ValuePenguin uninsured statistics). Uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage is optional, but it protects your right to pain-and-suffering compensation when the at-fault driver can't pay.

Is Michigan Still More Expensive Than At-Fault States?

Generally yes, though the reform brought prices down for many drivers who chose lower PIP levels. The trade-off is real. At-fault states like California, Texas and Colorado leave injury costs to liability insurers and lawsuits, while Michigan still guarantees significant first-party medical cover. Compare fault-free coverage options in PIP vs MedPay.

Real-Life Case Incident & Precedent
Precedent: Michigan No-Fault Act as amended by 2019 PA 21 (PIP choice levels; tort liability for excess economic loss); verbal threshold for non-economic damages

Illustrative Case: Two Grand Rapids Drivers, Two PIP Choices

Scenario: Two neighbours were each seriously hurt in separate crashes caused by other drivers. Each faced about $410,000 in medical and rehab costs in the first two years. Linda had kept unlimited PIP. Greg had switched to $250,000 PIP to save on his premium, and his employer health plan had high out-of-pocket costs and limited home-care benefits.

Resolution & Judicial Outcome: Linda's PIP paid her medical and rehab costs in full and keeps paying for future care. Greg's PIP stopped at $250,000. His health plan picked up part of the rest, and he sued the at-fault driver for the excess medical costs plus pain and suffering. The case depended on the other driver's $250,000 liability limit, with no guarantee of covering his future care.

What You Should Do: Step-by-Step Action Plan

1 Find your PIP medical level on your declarations page.
2 Before lowering PIP, confirm in writing what your health plan pays for car-crash injuries and long-term care.
3 Keep bodily injury liability at least at the 250/500 default.
4 Add UM/UIM to protect yourself from Michigan's many uninsured drivers.
5 Review your choices every year. Household changes affect which PIP options you qualify for.

Critical Mistakes to Avoid

  • Choosing the cheapest PIP option without checking your health plan's attendant-care and rehab limits.
  • Lowering liability limits below 250/500 to save money.
  • Opting out of PIP when a household member doesn't have qualifying coverage.
  • Forgetting the mini-tort claim for your vehicle damage deductible.
