Is Colorado a no-fault state?

IB
Insurance Bhaiya Auto Claims Group Verified Answer
Reviewed by Licensed Auto Claims Adjuster & Colorado Tort Specialist • Updated September 2026 • 6 min read
Zero Commission Bias
Quick Answer / Executive Summary

No. Colorado is an at-fault (tort) state. It used to be a no-fault state, but that system ended in 2003. Today the driver who causes a crash is responsible for injuries and damage through their liability insurance, and Colorado requires at least 25/50/15 liability. Insurers must include $5,000 of medical payments (MedPay) coverage unless you reject it in writing, which gives you fault-free help with medical bills. You can't recover damages if you're as much at fault as the other driver, or more.

Key Takeaways at a Glance
  • Colorado is an at-fault state. Its old no-fault PIP system expired in 2003, so articles describing Colorado PIP are out of date. Compare systems in no-fault vs at-fault car insurance states.
  • The minimum liability is 25/50/15, per the Colorado Division of Insurance.
  • $5,000 of MedPay is automatically part of your policy unless you reject it in writing (C.R.S. §10-4-635). It pays regardless of fault. See PIP vs MedPay.
  • Colorado bars recovery if your fault equals or exceeds the other driver's, so a 50/50 split means no recovery.
  • Colorado's lawsuit deadline for most car-crash injury claims is generally three years, longer than Texas and Georgia.

Is Colorado a No-Fault State? Not Since 2003

Colorado ran a no-fault system with mandatory personal injury protection (PIP) for decades. The law was allowed to expire on July 1, 2003, and Colorado switched to a tort (at-fault) system. That history is why you'll still find old forum posts and blogs describing 'Colorado PIP'. They're out of date.

How claims work in Colorado now:
• The at-fault driver's bodily injury and property damage liability pays for the other people's injuries and damage.
• You can claim against that insurer, use your own coverages first (MedPay, collision, UM) and let your insurer recover the money through subrogation, or sue.
• There's no injury threshold to sue, unlike no-fault states with a tort threshold.

Colorado sits alongside Texas and Georgia as an at-fault state, while New York keeps a strong no-fault system.
The MedPay Safety Net

When Colorado dropped PIP, it kept a fault-free option. Insurers must include $5,000 of medical payments coverage on every policy unless you reject it in writing. MedPay pays your and your passengers' medical bills straight away, regardless of who caused the crash. Keep it.

Colorado Car Insurance Requirements

Required liability (25/50/15):
• $25,000 bodily injury per person
• $50,000 bodily injury per crash
• $15,000 property damage

Included unless you reject it in writing:
• $5,000 medical payments (MedPay)
• Uninsured/underinsured motorist (UM/UIM), which insurers must offer

A $15,000 property damage limit is low. It may not cover one late-model SUV, let alone two cars. Anything above your limits can be pursued against you personally. See how to know if you are underinsured. Riders should also read how much is motorcycle insurance in Colorado. The same 25/50/15 minimum applies to motorcycles.
Don't Reject UM/UIM in Colorado

The Insurance Research Council estimates 1 in 3 US drivers were uninsured or underinsured in 2023. In an at-fault state, if an uninsured driver hits you, your own UM/UIM is the only policy paying for your injuries. See can an insurance company sue an uninsured driver.

How Fault Is Split in Colorado

Colorado uses modified comparative negligence. You recover damages reduced by your share of fault, but only if your fault is less than the other driver's (Bloomberg Law chart).

• 25% at fault: you recover 75%.
• 49% at fault: you recover 51%.
• 50% or more at fault: you recover nothing from the other driver.

That works in practice like Georgia's 50% bar, and it's slightly stricter than Texas's 51% bar. Evidence wins these cases: photos, dashcam footage, witness contacts and the police report. Be careful with recorded statements, and consider when to hire an insurance lawyer if fault is disputed.

What to Do After a Crash in Colorado

1. Get medical care and call police for any injury crash.
2. Photograph the scene, the vehicles and the road conditions (ice and snow come up in many Colorado fault disputes).
3. Open a MedPay claim with your own insurer for early medical bills.
4. File a third-party claim with the at-fault driver's insurer.
5. If they're uninsured, open a UM claim.
6. Track all expenses and keep the lawsuit deadline in mind.

Need your car fixed now? Your own collision coverage can pay straight away, minus the deductible, while your insurer chases the other side.

Real-Life Case Incident & Precedent
Precedent: Colorado modified comparative negligence (recovery barred when fault is equal or greater); C.R.S. §10-4-635 medical payments coverage

Illustrative Case: Emma's Icy Intersection in Denver

Scenario: Emma slid into an intersection on an icy January morning after another driver ran a stale yellow light. Her medical bills came to $8,700 and her car damage to $7,200. She had kept the $5,000 MedPay on her policy. The adjusters initially proposed a 50/50 fault split, citing her speed for the conditions.

Resolution & Judicial Outcome: Emma's MedPay paid $5,000 of her medical bills within weeks, regardless of fault. A nearby business's camera footage showed the other driver entering on red, and fault was revised to 70/30 in her favour. She recovered 70% of her remaining damages from the other driver's insurer. At the original 50/50 split, Colorado law would have barred her recovery entirely.

What You Should Do: Step-by-Step Action Plan

1 Confirm your policy still includes the $5,000 MedPay. If you rejected it, consider adding it back.
2 Raise liability above 25/50/15, especially property damage.
3 Keep UM/UIM matching your liability limits.
4 After a crash, collect video and witness evidence quickly. Fault splits decide Colorado claims.
5 Run your limits through the coverage gap checker.

Critical Mistakes to Avoid

  • Relying on outdated information that says Colorado has PIP.
  • Rejecting MedPay to save a few dollars.
  • Accepting a 50/50 fault split without challenging it.
  • Carrying only $15,000 of property damage in a state full of expensive trucks and SUVs.

Can an insurance company drop you or cancel your policy after you file a claim?

In most jurisdictions, an insurer cannot immediately cancel your policy mid-term solely because you filed a single legitimate claim, unless there was proven fraud, material misrepresentation, or non-payment. However, they are legally permitted to non-renew your policy when the term expires (typically every 6 or 12 months) by providing advance written notice.

What happens legally and financially if you get into a car crash without insurance?

Driving without insurance and causing an accident results in catastrophic personal financial liability and severe legal penalties. You are personally liable for 100% of all medical bills, property damages, and legal judgments out of pocket. Legally, authorities can suspend your driver's license, impound your vehicle, impose thousands in fines, and mandate SR-22 high-risk insurance for years.

Why did my car insurance premium increase even though I had no accidents or tickets?

Car insurance rates can increase substantially without any tickets or accidents due to macroeconomic rate revisions, rising vehicle repair costs, increased frequency of catastrophic weather losses in your zip code, insurance credit score changes, and widespread insurance industry underwriting adjustments across your state.

Does your personal auto insurance cover rental cars, or do you need rental counter coverage?

In the United States and Canada, your personal auto insurance coverage (comprehensive, collision, and liability) typically transfers directly to a rental car driven for personal pleasure, up to your existing policy limits and deductibles. However, personal policies almost never cover 'loss of use' administrative fees charged by rental companies, and do not provide coverage internationally.
