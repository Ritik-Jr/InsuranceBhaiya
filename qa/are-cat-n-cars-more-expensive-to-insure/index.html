Are Cat N cars more expensive to insure?

IB
Insurance Bhaiya Editorial Team
Updated October 2026 • 5 min read
Quick Answer / Executive Summary

Not necessarily. Many insurers price a repaired Cat N (non-structural write-off) car close to an equivalent clean car. One published comparison found a 2011 petrol Cat N car averaging £553 a year against £558 for the undamaged version. The catches are that some mainstream insurers refuse Cat N cars altogether, you must declare the category, and a future total loss pays out on the car's lower market value. Cat N cars typically sell for 20–40% less than clean equivalents. Cat S (structural damage) cars are harder to insure (see Cat S vs Cat N car insurance). For drivers with convictions, see our car insurance for high-risk and convicted drivers guide.

Key Takeaways at a Glance
  • Cat N means the insurer judged the repair uneconomical, but the damage was non-structural: cosmetic, electrical or mechanical, not the chassis.
  • Premiums are often similar to a clean car, but your choice of insurer is narrower, and specialists may beat mainstream quotes.
  • A future write-off pays the Cat N car's lower market value (actual cash value), not the clean-car value.
  • You must declare the Cat N status. Hiding it can void the policy.
  • If you already pay a conviction loading (see which insurance companies do not ask about criminal convictions), a write-off further narrows the insurers willing to quote.

What Cat N Actually Means

Since October 2017, UK insurers have classified write-offs under the ABI code: Cat A (scrap entirely), Cat B (break for parts; the body shell is crushed), Cat S (structural damage, repairable) and Cat N (non-structural damage, repairable). A Cat N car was declared a total loss because the repair cost was too high relative to the car's value, not because it's unsafe. Once properly repaired, it can go straight back on the road. Our Cat S vs Cat N comparison sets out the full differences.

Why Premiums Can Stay Close to a Clean Car

An insurer's main exposure on a car is the cost of repairs and the payout on a total loss. Because a Cat N car is worth less, a total loss costs the insurer less. That offsets the concern about the car's history. The result varies widely: some insurers add a loading, some charge the same as for a clean car, and some decline Cat N altogether. That's why shopping around matters more than usual. Start with our best rate on car insurance tips.
Bring Repair Evidence

Professional repair invoices, before-and-after photos, an independent engineer's report and a recent MOT can help specialists offer better terms on a Cat N car.

The Hidden Cost: Payout Value on the Next Claim

If a Cat N car is written off again, the insurer pays its market value as a Cat N car, which is typically 20–40% below a clean equivalent. You keep the purchase discount, but you never recover the clean-car price. If the car is financed, the gap between the payout and your loan balance can be significant. See gap insurance, and note that some gap insurers exclude previously written-off cars. Comprehensive cover (see comprehensive vs collision) still matters, because repair costs on Cat N cars can be high.

Cat N Cars for High-Risk and Convicted Drivers

If you already have a conviction loading, for example a DR10 (see how long do motoring convictions stay on your licence), you may need a specialist insurer that accepts both your conviction and a write-off. That's a smaller market than either alone. Compare total cost (purchase price plus insurance) over the years you'll own the car, as in Ryan's DR10 and Cat N scenario. US readers will recognise the same issue as 'salvage' or 'rebuilt' titles. Insurers covering these are often non-standard auto insurance carriers, and drivers who need an SR-22 insurance filing face the same narrowing of the market. If no carrier will cover you, there's an assigned risk auto insurance plan. How a DUI changes the numbers is covered in how much does car insurance go up after a DUI, and what you can legally keep off a UK application is in do you have to declare spent convictions.

Write-Offs, Bans and Refusals

Returning from a ban and buying a write-off at the same time? Read car insurance after a driving ban first. Some insurers decline write-offs altogether (see can an insurance company refuse to insure you). Telematics can offset part of the loading (see black box insurance for high-risk drivers), and points add their own uplift (see car insurance with points on your licence).
Real-Life Case Incident & Precedent
Precedent: ABI Code of Practice for the Categorisation of Motor Vehicle Salvage (2017 categories A, B, S, N).

Illustrative Case: The £4,000 Discount vs the Lower Payout

Scenario: Priya bought a Cat N 2019 Ford Fiesta for £7,800, against £11,800 for a clean equivalent, after a dealer repaired hail and electrical damage. Two mainstream insurers declined her. A specialist quoted £640 a year, against £610 for the clean car.

Resolution & Judicial Outcome: Her extra insurance cost was about £30 a year. Eighteen months later, the car was written off after a third-party collision, and the insurer paid £6,900, its Cat N market value. Overall she still came out ahead of buying the clean car, but she had to fund the difference to replace it.

What You Should Do: Step-by-Step Action Plan

1 Run a vehicle history check to confirm the category, and get the repair invoices before you buy.
2 Get insurance quotes before you pay: mainstream plus at least two specialists.
3 Declare the Cat N status on every quote.
4 Calculate total cost of ownership: purchase saving minus extra premium minus lower resale or payout value.
5 Check whether your gap insurer covers previously written-off cars if you're financing.

Critical Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not declaring Cat N status. The insurer can find out through salvage databases and void the policy.
  • Assuming a write-off will be paid out at clean-car value.
  • Buying a Cat S car on the assumption that it will insure like a Cat N.
