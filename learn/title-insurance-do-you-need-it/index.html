Title Insurance: Do You Need It? Lender's vs Owner's Policies, Costs and What They Cover

Title insurance explained: what lender's and owner's policies cover, what they cost at closing, who pays, and when an owner's policy is worth buying.

IB
Insurance Bhaiya Research Desk Peer-Reviewed
Actuarial Research Fellow • Updated October 2026 • 9 min read
100% Unbiased • No Kickbacks
30-Second Key Takeaways
  • Title insurance looks backwards: it protects against problems that already existed when you bought, such as unpaid liens, forged deeds and recording errors, not damage or events after closing.
  • There are two separate policies. A lender's policy is required by virtually all mortgage lenders and protects the loan; an owner's policy is optional and protects your equity.
  • Both are one-time premiums paid at closing. Combined they typically cost about 0.5% to 1% of the purchase price, with industry data putting the median near 0.67%.
  • Buying the owner's and lender's policies together from the same company usually earns a discount, and rates are set by the state in some places and open to shopping in others.
  • The lender's policy does not protect you. Without an owner's policy, you pay to defend or fix a title problem yourself.

Closing day brings a line on the settlement statement that most buyers have never heard of until it is in front of them. The question is title insurance worth it has a straightforward answer for most buyers, but the reasoning matters more than the verdict. This hub explains what a title policy protects, how the lender's and owner's versions differ, what you will pay, who pays it and when you can skip it. It links to a detailed answer for each question along the way.

What Title Insurance Does (and Does Not) Do

Before closing, a title company searches public records to confirm the seller truly owns the property and that nothing else is attached to it. Searches are thorough but not perfect: records can be misindexed, signatures forged and heirs overlooked. Title insurance covers the financial loss from defects that existed before your policy date and were not caught, including legal costs to defend your ownership. The detail is in what does title insurance cover.

Why It Is Different From Every Other Policy

Homeowners insurance protects against future events such as fire or storms, with premiums every year. Title insurance covers the past, is paid once and lasts as long as you or your heirs own the home. It is not a substitute for homeowners cover; check yours with the Home Insurance Coverage Calculator.

Lender's vs Owner's Policy

If you take out a mortgage, your lender will require its own policy. That policy covers only the lender's interest, up to the loan balance. It will not defend your ownership or repay your equity. An owner's policy is the one that protects you. What Title Insurance Costs and Who Pays

The premium is a single charge at closing. Combined lender's and owner's policies typically run about 0.5% to 1% of the purchase price, so on a $400,000 home that is roughly $2,000 to $4,000, with the industry median near 0.67% (about $2,680). The figures are illustrative; your total depends on the state, the price and the underwriter. Some states set rates by regulation, while elsewhere you can shop around. Who pays the owner's policy is also a matter of local custom and negotiation. The full breakdown is in how much does title insurance cost and who pays.

The Simultaneous-Issue Discount

If you buy the owner's policy at the same time and from the same underwriter as the lender's policy, the lender's premium is often heavily reduced. In some regulated states it can fall to a nominal amount. Ask for a quote with and without the owner's policy before you decide.

When an Owner's Policy Matters Most

  • Older homes with a long chain of owners, where there are more past documents to go wrong.
  • Foreclosures, estate sales and for-sale-by-owner purchases, where records and sellers can be less reliable.
  • Purchases from family or between related parties, where informal paperwork is common.
  • Buyers with a large equity stake or a small cash cushion, who could not absorb a legal fight.
  • Properties bought without a survey, where boundary issues are more likely to surface.

A cash buyer who accepts the risk can decline an owner's policy, but the saving is usually small compared with the loss it insures. Our worked example in is title insurance worth it puts numbers on that trade-off.

Buying After Closing and When You Refinance

An owner's policy is normally issued at closing. Buying one afterwards is sometimes possible, but it needs a fresh title search, depends on the insurer and state and may not cover problems that arose between closing and the policy date; see can you buy owner's title insurance after closing. When you refinance, the new lender will want its own lender's policy, but your existing owner's policy stays in force.

Title Protection Outside the United States

Title insurance is most common in the US. In England and Wales, solicitors or conveyancers investigate title and the Land Registry records ownership, with title indemnity policies used for specific known defects; read Alex's first-mortgage scenario for how a UK purchase unfolds. Canada uses title insurance alongside lawyer review. In civil-law countries, notaries typically play the central role. Local practice differs, so ask your conveyancer or lawyer what applies.

Your Closing-Day Protection Checklist

  1. Ask the title company for quotes for a lender's policy alone and for both policies together.
  2. Read the exceptions section of the title commitment so you know what the policy will not cover.
  3. Ask whether a survey or boundary endorsement is available for your lot.
  4. Negotiate who pays the owner's policy as part of the purchase contract.
  5. Check that your homeowners cover is sized to rebuild with the Home Replacement-Cost Estimator.
  6. Protect your ability to pay the mortgage as well as your ownership: title insurance guards the property, while the personal accident insurance guide explains how accident cover helps if an injury stops you working.
  7. Read your closing documents using how to read an insurance policy as a guide to spotting exclusions.

Common search queries answered with direct links to companion calculators and guides.

Is title insurance required?
A lender's policy is required by virtually all mortgage lenders. An owner's policy is optional. If you pay cash, no policy is typically required. See do I need owner's title insurance if my lender requires a policy.
Is title insurance worth it?
For most buyers an owner's policy is worth it because the one-time premium is small relative to the home's value and the cost of defending a title dispute. See is title insurance worth it for the worked example.
How much does title insurance cost?
Combined lender's and owner's policies typically cost about 0.5% to 1% of the purchase price, paid once at closing. Details and state differences are in how much does title insurance cost and who pays.
Can I buy title insurance after closing?
Sometimes. It needs a new title search and may not cover problems from the gap between closing and the policy date. See can you buy owner's title insurance after closing.
