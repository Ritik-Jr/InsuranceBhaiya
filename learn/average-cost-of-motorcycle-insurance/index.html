How Much Is Motorcycle Insurance? Average Cost by Age, Bike Type & State (2026)

How much is motorcycle insurance? See 2026 average costs for minimum and full coverage by age, bike type and state, plus the 7 factors that set your price.

IB
Insurance Bhaiya Research Desk Peer-Reviewed
Actuarial Research Fellow • Updated September 2026 • 10 min read
100% Unbiased • No Kickbacks
30-Second Key Takeaways
  • The national average cost of motorcycle insurance is about $141 a year for minimum coverage and $364 a year for full coverage, according to MoneyGeek's 2026 data.
  • Age matters enormously. A 16-year-old pays roughly $1,446 a year for full coverage, compared with about $571 at age 40, in the same dataset.
  • Bike type is the single biggest lever. Sport bikes can cost several times more than a standard or cruiser. IIHS finds supersport riders die at about 4 times the rate of cruiser and standard riders.
  • State matters too. Median premiums range from about $9 a month in Kansas and Montana to about $35 in New Jersey. See our Colorado and Ohio breakdowns.
  • Motorcycle insurance usually costs far less than car insurance, about 59% to 76% less on average, but it protects you far less in a crash. See motorcycle insurance vs car insurance.

If you're shopping for your first bike, or you've just seen a renewal quote that made you wince, you want one number: how much is motorcycle insurance, really? The honest answer is that it depends more than car insurance does. Two riders on the same street can pay $90 and $2,000 for the same year of cover. In this guide we give you the averages, show you exactly why your quote lands where it does, and explain how to bring it down without leaving yourself exposed. It's part of our Motorcycle, Dirt Bike & Powersport Insurance hub.

Motorcycle Insurance Cost by Age

Insurers price young riders the way they price young drivers, only more steeply. Crash data explains why: riders under 25 crash more, and motorcycle crashes are rarely minor. NHTSA reports that in 2024, per mile traveled, motorcyclists were almost 27 times more likely than car occupants to die in a crash. MoneyGeek's figures put full coverage at about $1,446 a year for a 16-year-old and about $571 a year for a 40-year-old. Rates usually fall steadily through the 20s and flatten out by your mid-30s, as long as your record stays clean.

Under 25? Here's What Actually Moves the Needle

Take a certified rider course (find one at the Motorcycle Safety Foundation). Start on a 300cc to 650cc standard instead of a supersport. Get your full endorsement rather than riding on a permit, which matters in can you get motorcycle insurance without a license. Consider a higher deductible. Want to see all four in action? To show how wide the gap can be, MoneyGeek's example priced a sport bike's full coverage at about $329 a month, against about $30 a month for a standard motorcycle with the same rider. Nothing else you control comes close to that effect.

Motorcycle Insurance Cost by State

Your state sets the legal minimum and your ZIP code sets your theft and traffic risk. Both show up in the premium. Median monthly premiums run from about $9 in Kansas and Montana to about $35 in New Jersey (MoneyGeek). ValuePenguin lists Kentucky, Florida, Arizona, Mississippi and Texas among the most expensive states. Theft is a big part of the story. The NICB ranks California, Florida and Texas as the top three states for motorcycle theft, and theft risk is priced straight into comprehensive coverage. Dig into specific states here:

  • How much is motorcycle insurance in Colorado? Minimum 25/50/15. About $117 a year minimum and $567 full coverage.
  • How much is motorcycle insurance in Ohio? Minimum 25/50/25. Roughly $115 to $315 a year minimum.
  • Do you need insurance on a motorcycle in Florida? Not required to register, but expensive to go without.

The 7 Factors That Set Your Motorcycle Insurance Price

  1. Bike type and engine size. The biggest factor by far.
  2. Age and riding experience. Years with a full endorsement count, not just your age.
  3. Riding and driving record. A DUI can raise full-coverage rates by around 68% (MoneyGeek). At-fault crashes linger for years. See how long does an accident stay on your insurance.
  4. Where you live and park. Urban street parking in a high-theft metro costs the most. How theft claims pay out is covered in does motorcycle insurance cover theft.
  5. Coverage choices. Liability-only is cheapest. Adding custom parts and equipment coverage, passenger cover and roadside assistance adds cost.
  6. Deductibles. Higher deductibles lower the premium. Find your break-even with the deductible vs premium calculator.
  7. Seasonality. Riders in cold states can save with lay-up coverage instead of paying for 12 months of full cover on a bike that sits for five.

How to Get Cheap Motorcycle Insurance Without Being Underinsured

There's a right way and a wrong way to cut your premium. The wrong way is dropping to state minimums when you have savings or home equity to protect, or skipping uninsured motorist coverage. Those are the coverages that pay for the worst day of your life. The right way looks like this:

  • Shop at least three quotes with identical limits. Prices for the same rider can vary by hundreds of dollars between insurers.
  • Bundle with your car or home policy. Multi-policy discounts are common.
  • Drop collision on an older bike once its value falls below roughly 10 times the annual collision premium.
  • Protect your passenger properly instead of over-buying extras. Read does motorcycle insurance cover passengers.
  • Financing a new bike? Price in gap insurance now. Find out why in the financed-bike total-loss scenario.
  • Check your limits against your net worth with the coverage gap checker.

Frequently Asked Questions & Related Resources

Common search queries answered with direct links to companion calculators and guides.

How much is motorcycle insurance a month?
On average, about $12 a month for minimum coverage and about $30 a month for full coverage, based on MoneyGeek's 2026 national data. Young riders and sport-bike owners often pay $100 to $300 a month or more.
Is motorcycle insurance cheaper than car insurance?
Usually, yes. Motorcycle insurance averages roughly 59% to 76% less than car insurance, depending on coverage level. It covers you less in a crash, though. See motorcycle insurance vs car insurance.
Why is my motorcycle insurance so expensive?
The usual culprits are a sport or supersport bike, being under 25, a recent ticket or crash, urban parking in a high-theft area, or a low deductible. Change any one of those and your quote can drop sharply.
Does motorcycle insurance cost less in winter?
It can, if your insurer offers lay-up coverage. This keeps comprehensive active while the bike is stored and pauses the riding coverages, so you pay less without a lapse.
