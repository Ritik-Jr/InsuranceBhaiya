How Often Should You Review Your Insurance? The Annual and Milestone Rule

Discover when and how often to review your insurance coverage: the annual policy audit rhythm and the 7 life milestone triggers that demand immediate policy updates.

IB
Marcus Vance
Actuarial Peer-Reviewed • Updated September 2026

Insurance is not a 'set-it-and-forget-it' financial product. As your life progresses, your assets grow, your liabilities change, and construction inflation erodes your dwelling coverage. Failing to review policies regularly is the primary cause of catastrophic underinsurance.

The Two Review Cadences

The 7 Life Milestones That Trigger a Policy Review

Life MilestoneAction RequiredRisk Prevented
Getting Married / DivorcedCombine auto policies for multi-car discount; update life insurance beneficiaries.Proceeds going to an ex-spouse or missing out on bundling discounts.
Welcoming a Baby / AdoptingIncrease term life coverage multiple; add newborn to health plan within 30 days.Losing health coverage special enrollment window or underinsuring child support.
Home Renovation / AdditionIncrease dwelling limit (Coverage A) on homeowner policy by remodel cost.Co-insurance penalties if home burns down after a $100k kitchen remodel.
Adding a Teenage DriverAdd teen to auto policy; raise liability limits and explore good-student discounts.Denied claims or catastrophic multi-million dollar liability lawsuits.
Starting a Home-Based BusinessAdd a home business endorsement; personal home insurance excludes commercial loss.Zero coverage if business inventory is stolen or a client slips on your steps.
Significant Pay Raise / Asset GrowthAdd an Umbrella Liability policy ($1M - $5M) to protect growing net worth.Lawsuit judgments seizing non-exempt investment and brokerage accounts.
Paying Off Your MortgageContact insurer to remove the bank as loss payee and verify continuing coverage.Inadvertent policy lapse if escrow payments cease.
Guidance from the Insurance Information Institute

The Insurance Information Institute (III) recommends using annual open enrollment periods to re-verify employer disability, life, and health plan coverage caps.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do I have to add a newborn baby to health insurance?
Most health plans provide a strict 30-day Special Enrollment Period following birth or adoption. If you miss this 30-day window, your baby cannot be added until the next annual open enrollment.

Can an insurance company drop you or cancel your policy after you file a claim?

In most jurisdictions, an insurer cannot immediately cancel your policy mid-term solely because you filed a single legitimate claim, unless there was proven fraud, material misrepresentation, or non-payment.
