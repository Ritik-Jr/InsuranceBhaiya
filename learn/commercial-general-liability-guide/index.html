Commercial General Liability (CGL): Third-Party Risk Management

A master primer on Commercial General Liability insurance for business owners: bodily injury, property damage, personal and advertising injury, and duty to defend.

IB
Devon Reed
Actuarial Research Fellow • Fact-Checked

Key Editorial Takeaways

  • Review your current declarations page to confirm exact deductibles, sub-limits, and named insured designations.
  • Policy exclusions supersede marketing summaries—always verify specific peril exceptions in Section I and II.
  • Adjust coverage whenever life circumstances change (e.g., mortgage refinancing, marriage, or acquiring high-value assets).

1. The Scope of Commercial General Liability

Commercial General Liability (CGL) protects a business against claims alleging that operations, products, or employees caused bodily injury or property damage to an outside third party (clients, vendors, visitors).

2. Coverage A, B, and C Explained

Coverage A covers Bodily Injury and Property Damage. Coverage B covers Personal and Advertising Injury (such as copyright infringement in advertising, slander, or false arrest). Coverage C provides no-fault Medical Payments for minor on-premises injuries without requiring litigation.

3. The Critical Duty to Defend

In addition to paying settlements or judgments up to the policy limit, CGL policies include the insurer's legal obligation to furnish defense counsel at the insurer's expense. Legal defense costs do not erode the policy limit in standard ISO CGL contracts.
