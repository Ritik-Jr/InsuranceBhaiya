Business Insurance Explained: What It Covers, What the Law Requires, and How Much Coverage Your Business Needs

Business insurance explained: which policies are legally required, what general liability and property really cover, theft and lawsuit gaps, limits and cancellation.

IB
Insurance Bhaiya Research Desk Peer-Reviewed
Actuarial Research Fellow • Updated October 2026 • 12 min read
100% Unbiased • No Kickbacks
30-Second Key Takeaways
  • Only a few coverages are legally required: workers' compensation once you have employees (nearly every state), unemployment insurance through payroll taxes, state disability insurance in a handful of states, and commercial auto liability for business vehicles. See who is exempt from workers' compensation insurance in California.
  • Everything else, including general liability, property, professional liability and crime, is required by contracts, landlords and lenders rather than by law.
  • No single policy covers every theft. Burglary falls under property insurance, employee theft under crime insurance, and stolen tools in transit under inland marine. See does business insurance cover theft.
  • General liability defends injury and property damage lawsuits; employment, professional and data breach suits need separate policies. See does business insurance cover lawsuits.
  • A $1 million per occurrence / $2 million aggregate general liability limit is the common contract benchmark, but the right amount depends on your assets and contracts. See how much business insurance do I need.

Most small business owners buy insurance the same way: a landlord or client asks for a certificate, a broker sends a quote for a 'general liability' or 'business owner's' policy, and the owner signs it without a clear picture of what it covers. Then something happens, such as an employee walking off with cash, a customer suing over a fall, or a boat sinking at the dock, and the owner finds out which part of the business was never insured. This hub maps the whole landscape: what the law requires, what each core policy pays for, where the common gaps sit, how to size your limits, and how to change or cancel coverage without leaving a hole.

The Most Expensive Mistake: Skipping Workers' Comp

In California, operating without required workers' compensation is a misdemeanor. The state can issue a stop order on your employee labor and assess a penalty of the greater of twice the premium you should have paid or $1,500 per employee. Check whether you qualify for an exemption in who is exempt from workers' compensation insurance in California before assuming you're exempt.

The Core Policies and What Each One Covers

  • General liability (GL): Bodily injury and property damage you cause to others, plus personal and advertising injury such as libel. It is the policy behind most 'is my business covered if someone sues' questions, answered in full in does business insurance cover lawsuits.
  • Commercial property: Your building, equipment, furniture and inventory against fire, storm, vandalism and burglary. Whether it pays for a specific loss, including theft, is explained in does business insurance cover theft.
  • Business owner's policy (BOP): A bundled GL plus property policy for smaller businesses, usually with business income coverage. It is cheaper than buying the two separately but has eligibility limits.
  • Professional liability (E&O): Claims that your advice, design or service caused a financial loss. General liability does not cover these; compare the two in general liability vs professional liability.
  • Commercial crime: Employee theft, forgery and certain fraud, which property policies exclude. See commercial crime insurance.
  • Cyber liability: Data breaches, ransomware and funds-transfer fraud.
  • Specialty lines: Businesses on or near water need marine coverages that ordinary policies exclude; see marine business insurance.
  • Commercial umbrella: Extra liability limits above GL, auto and employer's liability. Our umbrella insurance guide explains how the layers stack.

Each of these gaps comes from an exclusion, a clause that removes a risk from the policy. Reading those clauses before a loss is the single best use of an hour; our guide on what is an exclusion in insurance shows where to find them. The theft gaps are detailed in does business insurance cover theft, and the lawsuit gaps in does business insurance cover lawsuits.

How Much Coverage Is Enough

To determine specific coverage baselines and industry benchmarks, consult our dedicated Q&A on how much business insurance do I need.

Limits should be set by three numbers: what your contracts and lease demand, what it would cost to rebuild and restock after a total loss, and how much of your business and personal wealth a lawsuit could reach. For liability, $1 million per occurrence and $2 million aggregate is the common contract benchmark; for property, insure at replacement cost rather than depreciated value. The full sizing method, with worked examples, is in how much business insurance do I need, and you can estimate your figures with the Coverage Calculator.

Specialty Risks: Businesses on the Water

For commercial vessel and maritime operations, review essential maritime requirements in our Q&A on marine business insurance.

Marinas, boat dealers, charter operators, marine contractors and repair yards face risks standard policies simply exclude: customers' vessels in your care, liability from operating boats, fuel spills, and employees covered by federal maritime law instead of state workers' comp. That is why marine business insurance is its own category, and why marine employers can't rely on a California workers' comp policy alone to cover their crews, even after checking who is exempt from workers' compensation insurance in California. The federal options are explained in USL&H vs Jones Act coverage.

Changing or Cancelling Business Insurance

You can usually cancel a business policy at any time with written notice. The questions are how much premium you get back, whether a short-rate penalty applies, and whether cancelling breaks a contract that requires you to stay insured. The rules, and the safe order of operations, are in can you cancel business insurance at any time. Claims-made policies also need an extended reporting period (tail coverage) when you close or switch. For personal policies, see can you cancel insurance anytime and get a refund.

Your Business Insurance Checklist

  1. Confirm legal requirements for your state and headcount, starting with workers' comp exemptions (California rules here) and whether your 'contractors' really are (do independent contractors need workers' comp in California).
  2. Collect contract requirements from your lease, client agreements and lenders.
  3. List what could be stolen and by whom, then match each to a policy (does business insurance cover theft).
  4. List who could sue you and check which policy defends each type of claim (does business insurance cover lawsuits).
  5. Size your limits (how much business insurance do I need).
  6. Add specialty coverage if you operate near water (marine business insurance).
  7. Review annually, and never cancel before replacement coverage starts (can you cancel business insurance at any time).
  8. Run the Business Insurance Checklist to catch anything missed.

Frequently Asked Questions & Related Resources

Common search queries answered with direct links to companion calculators and guides.

What business insurance is required by law?
Workers' compensation once you have employees (in nearly every state), unemployment insurance through payroll taxes, state disability insurance in a few states, and auto liability for business vehicles. General liability is usually required by contracts, not law.
Does business insurance cover theft?
Commercial property covers burglary of your business property, but employee theft needs crime insurance and tools stolen in transit need inland marine coverage. Full breakdown: does business insurance cover theft.
Does business insurance cover lawsuits?
General liability covers injury, property damage and advertising injury lawsuits, including defense costs. Employment, professional-error and data-breach lawsuits need separate policies. See does business insurance cover lawsuits.
How much business insurance do I need?
Start with $1 million per occurrence / $2 million aggregate general liability, property at replacement cost, and enough business income coverage for a realistic rebuild period, then adjust for contracts and assets. See how much business insurance do I need.
Can you cancel business insurance at any time?
Usually yes, with written notice, but you may receive a short-rate refund rather than a full pro-rata refund. See can you cancel business insurance at any time.

Can an insurance company drop you or cancel your policy after you file a claim?

In most jurisdictions, an insurer cannot immediately cancel your policy mid-term solely because you filed a single legitimate claim, unless there was proven fraud, material misrepresentation, or non-payment.
