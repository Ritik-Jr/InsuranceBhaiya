State Auto Insurance Compliance Explained: Proof of Insurance, RV Rules, SR-22 Filings & State Programs in California and Florida

What California and Florida actually require: minimum limits, proof of insurance, RV rules, SR-22 and FR-44 filings, real state programs, and the ads that only pretend to be one.

30-Second Key Takeaways
  • California's minimum liability limits rose to 30/60/15 ($30,000 per person, $60,000 per accident, $15,000 property damage) on January 1, 2025. Read the full breakdown in our California evidence of liability insurance guide.
  • Florida requires only $10,000 Personal Injury Protection and $10,000 Property Damage Liability for most drivers, and that coverage must stay continuous for the whole registration period, even if the car never moves.
  • A 'Florida compliant drivers program' is not a state agency program. The real discounts come from the state-approved mature driver course, safe-driver rating and telematics. See what the Florida compliant drivers program really is.
  • Motorhomes in California must carry the same 30/60/15 liability as a car; towable trailers have no stand-alone mandate. Details in our California RV insurance guide.
  • SR-22 and FR-44 are filings, not policy types. An FR-44 forces Florida DUI offenders to carry 100/300/50 for at least three years. Compare them in SR-22 vs FR-44.

Most drivers think about car insurance as a price. The state thinks about it as a compliance record. California and Florida both run electronic systems that match every registered vehicle against an active policy, and when the two don't line up, the consequence isn't a higher premium. It is a suspended registration, a suspended license, a reinstatement fee, and in some cases a multi-year filing requirement that follows you to every insurer you call. This hub covers the compliance side of auto insurance in the two states our readers ask about most: what the law requires, how you prove it, how recreational vehicles fit in, which state programs are real, and which ones are marketing wearing a government costume.

Compliance Is Three Separate Things

When a state says you must be 'insured', it is actually checking three different obligations. Mixing them up is how people end up suspended while holding a perfectly valid policy.

  1. Carrying the minimum coverage. Your policy's limits must meet the state floor. In California that floor changed in 2025, so a policy written years ago at the old limits had to be updated at renewal.
  2. Proving it on demand. Police, the DMV and the other driver after a crash can all ask for evidence. California calls this 'evidence of financial responsibility' and accepts a short list of forms, which we walk through in our California evidence of liability insurance guide.
  3. Keeping it continuous. Both states receive electronic reports from insurers. A cancellation or non-payment lapse is reported automatically, and the state acts on the gap whether or not you were driving during it.

California vs Florida: The Minimums Side by Side

The two states take opposite approaches. California is a fault state that mandates bodily injury liability. Florida is a no-fault state that mandates Personal Injury Protection for your own injuries and property damage liability, but no bodily injury liability for most drivers.

Minimum Is a Legal Floor, Not a Recommendation

A $15,000 property damage limit will not replace most new cars, and $30,000 per person rarely covers a serious injury. Anything above your limit becomes a personal debt. Proof of Insurance: What Counts in California

California Vehicle Code section 16028 requires drivers to show evidence of financial responsibility when a police officer asks and after any collision. An insurance ID card, on paper or on your phone, is what nearly everyone uses. Separately, insurers must electronically report private-passenger vehicle coverage to the DMV under Vehicle Code section 16058, which is how the DMV knows the moment a policy is cancelled. Forgetting the card at a traffic stop has its own penalties, covered in what happens if you can't show proof of insurance in California, and a crash triggers a separate DMV report explained in do you have to report an accident to the California DMV. Drivers on tight budgets can stay legal through the state's genuine California Low Cost Auto Insurance Program. If you are selling a car, storing one, or switching insurers, the order of operations matters, and our California evidence of liability insurance walkthrough explains how to switch without triggering a suspension notice.

Florida: Real State Programs vs 'Program' Advertising

Search 'Florida compliant drivers program' and you will find ads promising a special state rate for drivers with clean records. No Florida agency runs a program by that name. The phrase is used by lead-generation sites that collect your details and sell them to agents. The genuine savings routes are less exciting but real: the state-approved mature driver course for drivers 55 and older (authorized by Florida Statutes section 627.0652, with a discount that typically lasts three years), safe-driver rating, and usage-based telematics. Our Florida compliant drivers program breakdown shows how to tell an insurer quote from a lead form. For the legal baseline, start with what is the minimum car insurance required in Florida, learn the deadlines that cut benefits in Florida PIP insurance explained, see how drivers 55+ claim the Florida mature driver insurance discount, and know the fees in what happens if car insurance lapses in Florida.

Why Fake 'Government Program' Ads Matter

In 2025 the Federal Trade Commission settled with lead generator MediaAlpha for $45 million over health insurance ads that faked an affiliation with the government. The same playbook appears in auto insurance. A real state program never needs your Social Security number on a landing page to 'check eligibility'.

When the State Makes Your Insurer Vouch for You: SR-22 and FR-44

After certain violations, carrying insurance is no longer enough; your insurer has to file a certificate with the state confirming you are covered and promising to notify the state if the policy ends. That certificate is the SR-22. Florida adds a second, stricter version, the FR-44, for DUI convictions, with limits of 100/300/50 that must be held for at least three years. The two are often confused, so we answer the difference directly in SR-22 vs FR-44: what is the difference. For a full breakdown of filing costs, durations, and non-owner policies, consult our definitive guide on SR-22 insurance explained, review options in car insurance for high-risk and convicted drivers, and learn how state-sponsored assigned risk auto insurance plans step in when commercial carriers decline coverage.

RVs: Where Auto Rules and Home-Style Coverage Meet

A motorhome is a motor vehicle, so in California it needs the same 30/60/15 liability as a sedan. A travel trailer or fifth wheel has no stand-alone insurance mandate; your tow vehicle's liability generally extends to it while it is hitched, but nothing pays for damage to the trailer itself unless you buy physical damage coverage. Full-time RVers need something closer to a homeowners policy on wheels. All of it is covered in our California RV insurance guide, including how comprehensive vs collision choices change when the vehicle is also your living room. If you tow, read does car insurance cover a travel trailer; if you live on the road, read full-time RV insurance.

The Other Compliance Bill California Homebuyers Meet: PMI

Compliance is not only a driving issue. Lenders impose their own insurance requirements, and the most expensive one for California buyers is private mortgage insurance on conventional loans with less than 20% down. Freddie Mac estimates PMI at roughly $30 to $70 a month for every $100,000 borrowed, and California loan sizes magnify that. Federal law sets clear points at which it must come off. Read California private mortgage insurance for the cancellation rules and the state's CalHFA assistance programs, and PMI vs FHA MIP if you are deciding between loan types.

Your Compliance Checklist

  • Check your declarations page against the current state minimum. Our guide on how to read an insurance policy shows where the limits sit.
  • Keep digital proof on your phone and a paper card in the glovebox for dead-battery days.
  • Never cancel before replacing. Start the new policy first, then cancel the old one, so the state's database never sees a gap.
  • Surrender or store properly. In Florida, surrender the plate before cancelling coverage on a car you no longer use; in California, use planned non-operation status rather than simply letting coverage lapse.
  • Treat 'state program' ads with suspicion unless the URL ends in .gov or the ad names a licensed insurer.
  • If you were ordered to file an SR-22 or FR-44, set a calendar reminder for the end date and confirm with the state before dropping the filing. See SR-22 vs FR-44.
  • Insure the RV separately if you own one, and confirm whether your trailer is covered while unhitched. See California RV insurance.

Frequently Asked Questions & Related Resources

Common search queries answered with direct links to companion calculators and guides.

What is the minimum car insurance required in California in 2026?
Liability limits of $30,000 per person and $60,000 per accident for bodily injury, plus $15,000 for property damage. These 30/60/15 limits took effect January 1, 2025. Full details are in our California evidence of liability insurance guide.
Does Florida require bodily injury liability insurance?
Not for most drivers. Florida requires $10,000 in Personal Injury Protection and $10,000 in Property Damage Liability. Bodily injury liability becomes mandatory through an SR-22 or FR-44 filing after certain violations, explained in SR-22 vs FR-44.
Is the Florida compliant drivers program a government program?
No. No Florida state agency operates a program by that name; it is a marketing phrase used by lead-generation websites. Real discounts come from the state-approved mature driver course and insurer safe-driver programs, covered in our Florida compliant drivers program guide.
Do I need insurance on a travel trailer in California?
California does not require separate insurance on a towable trailer. Your tow vehicle's liability usually extends to it while hitched, but damage to the trailer itself is only covered if you buy physical damage coverage. See our California RV insurance guide.
