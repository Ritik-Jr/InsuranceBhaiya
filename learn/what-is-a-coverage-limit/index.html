What Is a Coverage Limit? Per-Occurrence, Aggregate, and Sub-Limits

Understand insurance coverage limits: the maximum dollar amount an insurer will disburse, per-occurrence vs aggregate caps, split limits in auto, and internal sub-limits.

IB
Marcus Vance
Actuarial Research Fellow • Fact-Checked

Key Editorial Takeaways

  • Review your current declarations page to confirm exact deductibles, sub-limits, and named insured designations.
  • Policy exclusions supersede marketing summaries—always verify specific peril exceptions in Section I and II.
  • Adjust coverage whenever life circumstances change (e.g., mortgage refinancing, marriage, or acquiring high-value assets).

A coverage limit is the absolute maximum dollar ceiling an insurer is legally bound to disburse for a covered loss under the terms of your insurance policy. Any loss or legal judgment that exceeds this limit becomes your personal out-of-pocket liability.

Per-Occurrence vs. Aggregate Limits

Understanding Split Limits in Auto Policies (e.g., 250/500/100)

Auto insurance liability limits are universally quoted as three numbers separated by slashes. Here is what they signify:

Split Limit ComponentWhat It CoversExample Dollar Cap (250/500/100)
Bodily Injury Per PersonMax medical and legal damages paid for any single injured individual$250,000
Bodily Injury Per AccidentMax total medical and legal damages paid for all injured people combined$500,000
Property Damage LiabilityMax property damage paid for third-party vehicles, fences, and guardrails$100,000
Beware of Hidden Sub-Limits

Standard homeowner policies contain internal sub-limits for high-value theft items. Even if you hold a $300,000 personal property limit, the policy likely caps jewelry theft at $1,500 and firearms at $2,500 unless you purchase a scheduled endorsement.

Frequently Asked Questions

How can I protect my assets beyond my policy limits?
Purchase an Umbrella Liability Policy. For $200 to $350 per year, an umbrella policy adds $1,000,000 to $5,000,000 of excess liability coverage over your auto and home policies.
Review policy limits at every major life milestone. Guidance provided by the [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/).
