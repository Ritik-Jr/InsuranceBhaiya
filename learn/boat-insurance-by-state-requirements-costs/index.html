Boat Insurance by State: Requirements & Costs

Is boat insurance actually required in your state, what does it cost, and which specific boats need a different kind of policy entirely — answered question by question.

IB
Insurance Bhaiya Boat & Marine Insurance Group Peer-Reviewed
Actuarial Research Fellow • Updated September 2026 • 24 min read
100% Unbiased • No Kickbacks
30-Second Key Takeaways
  • Boat insurance is not legally required for most recreational boat owners in most US states — only a small number of states, Arkansas among them, mandate liability coverage, and even then usually only above a specific engine horsepower threshold.
  • Even where it's not required by state law, a lender financing your boat and a marina renting you a slip will almost always require proof of insurance as a condition of the loan or the lease, which functions as a practical requirement for most owners.
  • Average recreational boat insurance costs commonly range from roughly $200 to $500 a year for a typical boat, though this varies enormously by boat type, value, state, and usage — see our full cost breakdown for the specific factors.
  • A standard recreational boat policy usually doesn't extend to renting the boat out, running boat tours, ferrying paying passengers, storing boats for sale, or living aboard full-time — each of those needs its own specific policy type, covered in the linked spokes below.
  • Marina insurance (covering the docks and marina property itself) is a completely different product from insuring the boats that use the marina — see our dedicated Marina Insurance guide for that side of the equation.

"Do I need boat insurance?" doesn't have one national answer — it genuinely depends on your state, your boat's engine size, whether it's financed, and where you keep it. This guide walks through what's actually required where, what it costs, and which specific situations (a classic boat, a houseboat, a dealership, a charter business) need an entirely different kind of policy than a standard recreational boat owner.

Is Boat Insurance Legally Required?

In most US states, no — there's no blanket state law requiring recreational boat owners to carry insurance, which surprises a lot of people used to auto insurance being mandatory almost everywhere. Arkansas is a notable exception: under Arkansas Code § 27-101-207, boats with an engine over 50 horsepower must carry liability insurance, a requirement confirmed directly in the state's official boater education handbook. Utah has a broadly similar horsepower-based mandate. Beyond these specific exceptions, most states leave the insurance decision to the owner, the lender, and the marina — not the state itself. This also means a meaningful share of boaters on the water carry no insurance at all — see What Is Uninsured Boater Coverage? for how to protect yourself against that specific gap.

So Why Do So Many Boat Owners Carry Insurance Anyway?

Even in a state with no legal mandate, two practical forces push most owners toward carrying coverage: a bank or credit union financing the boat purchase will almost always require comprehensive and collision coverage as a loan condition, the same way an auto lender does, and a marina renting out a slip will typically require proof of liability insurance before granting dockage — see our Marina Insurance guide for the marina's own side of that requirement. On top of that, a serious boating accident can create real financial and even legal exposure regardless of whether insurance was technically mandatory beforehand.

Requirements Change — Always Verify Directly

Boating laws are set and updated at the state level. Before assuming your state has no requirement, confirm current rules directly with your state's boating registration authority (often the state's Department of Natural Resources, Parks and Wildlife Department, or equivalent) rather than relying solely on this table.

How Much Does Boat Insurance Actually Cost?

Recreational boat insurance commonly runs somewhere between roughly $200 and $500 a year for a typical mid-size boat, though the real number depends heavily on the boat's type, age, and value, where it's used and stored, and the coverage limits chosen. A full breakdown of what actually drives that price — and how it differs for larger, high-value, or high-risk vessels — is covered in How Much Does Boat Insurance Actually Cost? Coastal and hurricane-exposed states carry a specific added cost factor of their own — see Boat Insurance and Hurricane Season — and choosing an adequate liability limit is a separate decision from the premium itself, covered in How Much Boat Liability Insurance Do You Actually Need?

Does a Standard Boat Policy Cover Every Situation?

No — a standard recreational boat policy is built around personal, non-commercial use, and several common situations fall outside it entirely, each needing its own specific coverage:

  • Commercial Boat Insurance — for charters, tours, ferrying passengers, or any boat used to generate income
  • Classic Boat Insurance — for antique, wooden, or collector vessels valued differently than a standard boat
  • Houseboat Insurance — for a vessel used as a full- or part-time residence, which blends marine and home insurance concepts
  • Boat Dealers Insurance — for businesses that sell, display, or transport boats they don't personally own
  • Marina Insurance — for the dock, storage yard, and marina property itself, separate from any individual boat's coverage
  • Personal Watercraft (Jet Ski) Insurance — for jet skis and other PWC, which are priced and often legally treated differently than boats
  • Does Boat Insurance Cover Your Trailer? — for the practical split between your boat and auto policy
  • Uninsured Boater Coverage — for protection when the other boater in an accident has no insurance of their own
  • How Much Boat Liability Insurance Do You Actually Need? — for choosing a limit beyond any state minimum
  • Boat Insurance and Hurricane Season — for named storm deductibles and hurricane plan requirements

Frequently Asked Questions & Related Resources

Common search queries answered with direct links to companion calculators and guides.

Is boat insurance mandatory everywhere in the US?
No. Only a small number of states, including Arkansas, have a specific legal mandate, and it's typically tied to engine horsepower rather than applying to every boat. Most states leave the decision to the owner, though lenders and marinas frequently require it in practice.
Do I need boat insurance to register a boat?
In most states, no — boat registration is generally a separate process from insurance, and proof of insurance usually isn't required to register a recreational vessel, unlike car registration in many states.
Does my homeowner's insurance cover my boat?
Sometimes, but usually only for very small boats (often under a specific horsepower or value threshold) and only for limited coverage. Most boats, especially anything with meaningful horsepower or value, need a dedicated boat policy rather than relying on a homeowner's policy.
