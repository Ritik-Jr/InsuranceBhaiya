What Is an Insurance Claim? Step-by-Step Claims Process and Payouts

Understand the insurance claims process from start to finish: First Notice of Loss (FNOL), adjuster investigations, repair estimates, deductible subtractions, and payouts.

IB
David Sterling
Actuarial Research Fellow • Fact-Checked

Key Editorial Takeaways

  • Review your current declarations page to confirm exact deductibles, sub-limits, and named insured designations.
  • Policy exclusions supersede marketing summaries—always verify specific peril exceptions in Section I and II.
  • Adjust coverage whenever life circumstances change (e.g., mortgage refinancing, marriage, or acquiring high-value assets).

An insurance claim is a formal, legal request submitted by a policyholder to an insurance carrier requesting financial reimbursement or defense for a covered loss or liability incident.

Documentation Is Your Greatest Asset

Following property damage or an auto crash, immediately take timestamped photographs and video of all damaged property before initiating cleanup or discarding ruined items.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Public Adjuster?
A Public Adjuster is an independent licensed claims professional hired by the policyholder (not the insurance company) to advocate and negotiate for a larger settlement in complex property claims, typically charging 10% to 15% of the payout.
Claims handling regulations and statutory prompt-payment deadlines are enforced by state insurance commissioners.
